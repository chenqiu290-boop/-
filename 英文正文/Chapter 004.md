# Chapter 4: Dou the Eleventh

Behind the side door was a narrow passage squeezed between two high walls.

At the end of the passage, everything opened up.

A long covered walkway wrapped around a courtyard. Its pillars were lacquered vermilion, and freshly washed hangings were draped over the railings, still dripping. Two maids came toward them from the far end carrying food trays. When they saw Manager Fan, they lowered their heads and stepped aside, and only moved on once the party had passed. Somewhere off in one of the inner courtyards, someone was playing the pipa, haltingly; every time they hit a wrong note, they stopped and started over from the top.

The air smelled of incense, and of cooking smoke drifting over from the kitchen, with a whiff of horse dung mixed in. On the open ground beyond the walkway, a Hu groom was brushing down a chestnut horse. Every stroke of the brush got a snort out of the horse.

Walking under the eaves, Shen You did a quiet estimate.

This corridor alone was longer than the apartment he'd rented back in the modern world.

Manager Fan led them through two more gates, deeper and deeper into the out-of-the-way parts of the house. The courtyards kept getting smaller, the lacquer on the pillars older. At last they entered a small side courtyard. An old pear tree grew there, and beneath it stood a wooden perch.

On the perch crouched a falcon.

A young man stood in front of it, his left forearm wrapped in a thick leather arm guard, a strip of raw meat pinched in his right hand, teasing the bird.

He was in his early twenties, dressed in a moon-white robe of figured silk. From the *diexie* belt at his waist hung a small knife, a tally pouch, and a little fish-shaped jade pendant. There was a bruise on one cheekbone, as if something had knocked into him.

"Eleventh!" A gray-haired old woman came hurrying out of the house after him with a bowl of soup noodles. "Your noodles have gone all gluey!"

"Leave it, leave it." The young man didn't turn around.

"Leave it, leave it. Yesterday it was leave it, the day before it was leave it..." Grumbling, the old woman set the bowl down hard on the stone table. Then she noticed the strangers in the courtyard, shot Manager Fan a glare, and went back inside.

The young man fed the strip of meat to the falcon. Only then did he turn around. His eyes passed over the three of them and came to rest on Shen You's face.

"Fan Da. These the ones?"

"Yes." Manager Fan stepped forward. "That young fellow there. In a little over a double-hour, he won three times his stake. Wins three, loses one, and only ever loses on small bets. Never once bet against Wang the Eldest."

The young man raised an eyebrow. "Who's Wang the Eldest?"

"A farmer from south of the city. Sold his donkey to come gamble."

"Ah." The young man glanced at Zhou Two. "Won't take donkey money. A man with standards."

He cupped his hands to Shen You with an easy smile.

"Dou the Eleventh, of the Dou family. Fan Da didn't give you a fright, inviting you here, I hope?"

"Not at all." Shen You returned the salute. "Manager Fan invited us very politely."

"He's always polite." Dou the Eleventh grinned. "And when polite doesn't work, he stops being polite."

***

There was no one else in the courtyard. Dou the Eleventh had folding stools brought out, had the soup noodles cleared off the stone table, and had a plate of pears set down in their place.

"From our own tree. Sour. You'll have to make do."

Shen You took one and bit into it.

It really was sour.

Dou the Eleventh watched him wince and gave a laugh. Then he turned to Zhou Two. "Fan Da says you throw a good game of *chupu*. Throw a couple for me?"

A maid brought out a wooden tray and five wooden pieces.

Zhou Two picked up the pieces and weighed them in his hand, then turned them over one by one to look.

"Young master's pieces are better than the ones in the other houses."

"Fan Da, did you hear that?" Dou the Eleventh was delighted. "Even he says the pieces in your house are no good."

Manager Fan's face didn't move. "The ones out there, nobody minds if they get thrown to ruin."

Zhou Two said nothing more. He gathered the five pieces into his palm and scattered them with a careless toss.

The pieces rolled around the tray a few times and stopped.

All five black.

*Lu.*

He picked them up and threw again.

*Lu* again.

The third throw, the same.

The courtyard went quiet for a while. Even the falcon on its perch cocked its head and stared at the wooden tray.

Dou the Eleventh didn't cheer. He reached over, took the five pieces, and turned them over one by one, examining them closely.

"My pieces. My tray. My courtyard." He set them down and looked at Zhou Two. "How did you do it?"

Zhou Two yawned. "Practice."

Dou the Eleventh stared at him for a long moment. Then, all at once, he threw back his head and roared with laughter.

***

When he was done laughing, he turned to Shen You.

"Fan Da says you live south of the city. In the ward that grows millet."

"That's right."

"And what is it you do?"

"I was a monk. Returned to lay life."

"A monk?" Dou the Eleventh's gaze dropped and landed on Shen You's hands. "Monks haul water, sweep floors, split firewood. Your hands are softer than mine."

Shen You looked down at his hands.

Seven years of pounding a keyboard, and not one callus on his fingers.

"I copied sutras at the temple," he said.

"Copied sutras," Dou the Eleventh said, unhurried. "A sutra copier wears a callus into the middle finger of his right hand. We've got two old servants at home who copy sutras. Their calluses are hard enough to scrape a layer of skin off you."

Shen You raised his right hand and turned it over so the man could see.

The middle finger was perfectly smooth.

He laughed first. "Then I was a lazy sutra-copying monk."

Dou the Eleventh blinked. Then he was laughing again, slapping his knee.

"Good. Good." He waved a hand. "In Chang'an, who doesn't have a past he can't quite account for? I won't ask."

He tossed a pear into the air and caught it.

"So let's talk business. This young fellow. Name your price, and he's mine."

"He's not for sale."

"There's nothing in Chang'an that isn't for sale. Only prices that aren't high enough."

"Then name one that's high enough."

"Fifty strings."

Manager Fan's eyelid twitched.

Shen You didn't take up the number. Instead he asked, "What do you want him for?"

"To win money for me."

"Your house doesn't bet against its guests. It only takes a cut," Shen You said. "Whatever he wins in your house is your guests' money. Once the guests are scared of losing, they stop coming. And if the guests stop coming, who are you taking a cut from?"

Dou the Eleventh set the pear down.

"Go on."

"Have him sit in as your house player," Shen You said. "Once every five days, he comes and sits a session at your house. You put the word out among your regulars: there's a young fellow from the West Market who's never lost at *chupu*. Anyone who can beat him in half of ten throws, the house pays out ten times over."

"Chang'an gamblers can't stand a thing like that. The rich ones won't swallow it, the proud ones won't swallow it, and the ones who've lost will swallow it least of all. They'll come back again and again, and every time they'll bet bigger. The bigger they bet, the bigger your cut."

"What he wins goes to me. The extra cut goes to you. You don't spend a single coin, and you get a sign to hang out front for free."

Dou the Eleventh didn't say anything. His fingers tapped on his knee, one beat at a time.

"Hang too loud a sign," he said slowly, "and you draw people you don't want. The county constables have very good noses."

"So it only goes around among the regulars. It doesn't go any further," Shen You said. "The harder a house is to get into, the more people want in."

Dou the Eleventh's fingers stopped.

He looked at Shen You for a while, then glanced at Zhou Two.

"And if somebody really does beat him in half?"

Zhou Two didn't so much as lift his eyelids. "Then I pay the tenfold."

Dou the Eleventh was silent for a moment. Then he slapped his knee.

"Deal."

***

"One more thing," Dou the Eleventh went on. "How many more people like this have you got around you?"

"Just a few, for now."

It wasn't a lie. For now, there really were just a few. Tomorrow there'd be ten.

"If there are more down the line," Dou the Eleventh said, "come to me first."

Shen You smiled.

"Every sharp man in Chang'an seems to love that line."

"Who else has said it?"

"A Hu merchant."

Dou the Eleventh thought about it and didn't press. He just smiled. "Then I'll take second place in line."

Shen You bit into his sour pear, studied the young man, and suddenly asked, "You've got halberds racked outside your gate, and you're short of this little bit of money?"

Dou the Eleventh had been reaching for a strip of meat on the falcon's perch. His hand paused.

"The halberds are my uncle's." He held the strip out to the falcon, his tone perfectly ordinary. "I'm a concubine's son, eleventh in line, and my father went early. In this house, I eat at my uncle's table."

The falcon snatched the meat and swallowed it with a jerk of its neck.

"The Dou family is the new emperor's mother's clan. On the surface, nobody would ever slight us. But the face goes to my uncle and his lot. It never trickles down as far as me." He wiped his hands. "These days the Zhangsun family's threshold is nearly trampled flat. All of Chang'an is squeezing toward the new doors. I can't squeeze in, so I have to find my own road."

The whole time he talked, the smile never left his face, as if he were telling some other family's story.

Shen You didn't ask anything more.

It struck him, suddenly, that the pear tree in this courtyard, the falcon, the gluey soup noodles, and that old woman chasing out after him to scold were all far more real than the row of long halberds out front.

***

Manager Fan saw them out.

Walking down that narrow passage, Shen You glanced at the scar on his brow. "That scar's got some years on it, Manager."

"Under the walls of Huoyi. One of Song Laosheng's men." Manager Fan said it flatly. "That was the year I followed the Eleventh's father out of Taiyuan."

"And after that?"

"After that, his father died at Qianshuiyuan."

Shen You's step slowed by half a beat.

Qianshuiyuan. The one great defeat of Li Shimin's whole life. Five or six out of every ten Tang soldiers had died there.

"The Eleventh was only thirteen that year," Manager Fan said. "I promised his father I'd look after him."

He pushed open the side door, stopped, and looked at Shen You.

"The Eleventh may look careless, but there's no one clearer-headed. If he'd caught you lying to his face twice today, young master, you wouldn't be walking out this door."

"He caught me once."

"That once, you owned up to it yourself." Manager Fan cupped his hands. "Take care on your way, young master."

***

By the time they left the Dou house, the sun was already slanting west.

The three of them hurried south. The ward gates shut at dark, and from here to Kaiming Ward meant crossing most of Chang'an.

Afternoon Chang'an looked different again from the morning. The gong for the West Market's close sounded from far off. Camels carrying empty pack frames plodded toward Jinguang Gate, their bells clanking one note at a time, lazy and slow. The yellow earth of Vermilion Bird Avenue had been baked pale by the sun, and whenever a cart or horse went by, the dust it raised drifted golden in the slanting light and took forever to settle. The wind set the seedpods on the pagoda trees rattling.

By the roadside, an old man was hurrying into his ward with a load of firewood on a shoulder pole, the pole creaking under the weight. A woman riding a donkey wore a veiled hat; the wind lifted a corner of the gauze, and she reached up and pressed it back down.

As they neared Daxingshan Temple, the evening bell began to toll.

Shen You walked in the middle, Zhou Two on his left, Zhou Four behind. Zhou Four had three strings of coins and two bolts of silk on his shoulder and had been carrying them all afternoon, and his breathing hadn't changed at all.

"Master." Zhou Four spoke up suddenly, his voice low. "Someone's been following since we left the Dou house. Still there."

"One of Manager Fan's?"

"Walks like one."

Zhou Two asked, "Lose him?"

Shen You thought about it and shook his head. "Let him follow."

"A ruined temple, six people, one clay pot." He smiled. "Let him go back and report that. The more clearly he reports it, the less sleep our friend the Eleventh is going to get."

***

They made it into Kaiming Ward just before the gates closed.

A bitter smell hung around the door of the ruined temple. Zhou Six was crouched by the fire, keeping watch over the clay pot, where agastache and dried tangerine peel bubbled away.

Zhou Three and Zhou Five were back too.

"Blacksmith at the West Market says the wok'll take three days," Zhou Three said, sorting greens as she talked. "Price he's asking, it'd make your heart ache."

Zhou Five sat on the threshold, looking thoroughly unimpressed. "That bellows of his leaks like a sieve. Can't get the fire hot enough, so any wok he turns out'll be nothing special. Give me a few boards and I'll make one three times better than his."

"Then make it," Shen You said.

Zhou Six ladled out a bowl of the medicine and held it out to him. "Drink it while it's hot, Master."

Shen You took it, sniffed it, screwed up his face, and downed it in one go.

It was so bitter it took him a long while to recover.

"One more bowl tomorrow and you'll be fine." Zhou Six took the bowl back. Her face showed nothing much, but there seemed to be the faintest trace of a smile in her eyes.

Zhou One came over last, holding a few sheets of paper in both hands.

"Master. It's done."

A travel pass and a document of identity. The paper was old, its edges faintly yellowed, as if it had been pressed at the bottom of a chest for years. The writing was neat regular script, and at the end sat the vermilion stamp of a county seal, the ink uneven in color, as though it had been stamped long ago and had faded.

"Native of Chengdu County, Yizhou. Lay surname Shen, given name You. Left home as a child; copied sutras at a temple in the capital. Temple abolished in the fifth month of the ninth year of Wude; returned to lay life."

Shen You read it through word by word, then turned it over to check the back.

"Looks real."

"It is real," Zhou One said. "It's only that the registers at the county office don't have this page yet."

Shen You folded the document carefully and tucked it into the front of his robe.

From today on, he had a name in the Great Tang.

***

Early the next morning, in that small side courtyard of the Dou house.

The man who had tailed them all the way stood under the pear tree and reported everything, point by point. "Kaiming Ward, south of the city. A ruined temple. Leaks when it rains, doesn't even have a door. Besides the monk, there are six people in there, and one clay pot. They're still boiling medicine."

Dou the Eleventh sat under the eaves, turning the five wooden pieces over in his hand, and for a long while said nothing.

"Lives in a ruined temple," he murmured. "Has a gambler who's never lost, a guard who can carry three strings of coins all afternoon, and his own hands are softer than mine..."

He tossed the pieces carelessly into the tray.

Three black, two white.

"Interesting."

The old woman's voice came from inside the house. "Eleventh! Your breakfast's getting cold again!"

"Coming, coming."
