# Chapter 1: First Day on the Job

Shen You's greatest wish in life was to wake up one morning and not have to go to work.

Today, the wish came true.

The catch: he no longer seemed to be in the twenty-first century.

***

He woke up to a dog licking his face.

A yellow dog, so scrawny you could count every rib, squatting by his head and making a careful study of the last-night's-coffee smell on his face.

Shen You and the dog stared at each other for three seconds.

The dog blinked first. It tucked its tail and bolted.

He sat up, a few strands of dry grass stuck to the back of his head. All around him rose waist-high wild mugwort; past that ran row after row of vegetable beds, a few burial mounds half caved in, and a stretch of low rammed-earth wall leaning crookedly in the setting sun. The wind smelled of woodsmoke and livestock dung.

No office towers. No delivery scooters. No mechanical keyboard clacking away forever at the next desk over.

His work badge still hung around his neck. Blue lanyard. The guy in the photo had two dark circles under his eyes and was smiling like someone was holding a gun on him.

Now Shen You remembered. Last night: deploy, roll back, deploy again, roll back again, all the way to three in the morning. He'd said he would put his head down for a bit. Ten minutes, tops.

And then he was here.

He took the badge off, gave it a couple of looks, and hung it on a branch of the crooked jujube tree beside him.

"Guess I can skip the resignation letter."

***

An ox cart came creaking toward him from somewhere off in the distance. The driver was an old farmer with a cloth wrapped around his head, washed so many times it had faded to white, and a dusty gray short tunic of coarse cloth on his back.

The old man spotted him from a long way off. The cart slowed, and his eyes went over Shen You several times: first the strange clothes, then the head.

They stopped on the head.

Shen You ran a hand over his buzz cut and understood.

Every man in this era grew his hair long and tied it up in a topknot. A man with hair barely an inch long was either a convict, or a Hu foreigner, or else...

"Little Master," the old farmer said, stopping his cart three paces away and coming no closer, "which temple are you from?"

...or a monk.

Shen You's mind turned it over in an instant. He didn't deny it. He pressed his palms together and smiled.

"You've got sharp eyes, Elder."

The words were barely out of his mouth before he caught himself.

That wasn't Mandarin. The accent was strange, the syllables short and clipped, yet it rolled off his tongue as if he'd been speaking it for twenty-odd years. And he understood every word the old man said, too.

Transmigration, with a free language pack thrown in. Whoever ran customer support on this deal had really thought of everything.

Seeing him own up to it, the old farmer eased a little and sighed. "Back in the fifth month the court ordered a culling of monks and nuns. Half the temples in the capital came down just like that, and there were monks turned out on every street. Then in the sixth month they said never mind, it's off. But some temples were already torn down to nothing. There was nowhere to go back to." He looked Shen You's T-shirt up and down. "That getup of yours, some Hu merchant gave it to you as alms, I'd guess?"

"Something like that."

"Your hair's grown back an inch already. Two or three hard months, then." The old man shook his head. "Still, your timing's good. Just this noon the officials posted the proclamation. The new emperor's taken the throne, and there's a general amnesty for all under Heaven. Maybe things'll be a little easier from here on."

He glanced back at the few bundles of millet stalks in the cart and sighed again. "An amnesty pardons crimes, not taxes. Lots of sparrows this year. Once the grain tax and the cloth levy are paid, what's left will see us to spring, and that's all."

The new emperor.

Shen You's heart skipped a beat, but his face gave nothing away. "I've been drifting a while out there, lost track of the days. Elder, what's today's..."

"The ninth year of Wude, the ninth day of the eighth month." The old man said it slowly and clearly, as though worried he might forget. "Hold on to that. Likely as not they'll be changing the era name before long."

He pointed his whip east. "Kaiming Ward here, and the wards south of it, there's hardly a household in any of them. Once the ward gates close for the night, if you're wandering around the wasteland by yourself and the ward warden runs into you, he'll have questions. There's a ruined temple over that way. Used to be a little one, nobody there for ages now. Go make do there for the night."

He paused, then lowered his voice. "And another thing. Stay away from the north these days. My eldest is in the militia. Came home from his rotation last month and said the Turks are stirring up trouble again. They're moving troops in several prefectures up north."

The ox cart creaked off into the distance.

Shen You watched the old man's back go and thought: he'd taken Shen You for someone he wasn't, and yet from start to finish, he hadn't wasted a single word.

He'd asked what needed asking and warned about what needed warning. Even stopping three paces short and coming no closer had been calculated.

***

Shen You was in no hurry to get to the temple.

Behind the low wall was an earthen slope. He climbed it and looked north.

The south end of the city sat on high ground, and from up here the whole city lay spread out at his feet.

Yellow rammed-earth walls ran dead straight, crossing at right angles, cutting the city into squares, block after block, on and on past where the eye could follow. Between the walls ran perfectly straight avenues. The one down the middle was absurdly wide, as if someone had laid a ruler on the ground and scored a single line from the wasteland under his feet all the way to the horizon.

On the horizon stood a taller wall still. Behind it, rooftops rose tier upon tier, glowing red-gold in the setting sun. That was the Imperial City. Farther north lay the Palace City.

Closer in, a few thin threads of cooking smoke rose from behind the ward walls, sparse and scattered. Great swaths of the wards were nothing but millet fields, the heads already gone yellow and hanging heavy. Down below the slope, on the crooked jujube tree, the jujubes were half red and half green, and his badge swung among the branches.

Shen You looked for a long time.

He'd seen the reconstructions in videos. He knew the Sui had built this city to Yuwen Kai's plan: 108 wards, an East Market and a West Market, laid out like a chessboard. But reconstructions were dead things.

This one was alive. Someone was cooking supper behind a ward wall. A dog was running along a field ridge. An ox cart was creaking its way home. Somewhere far off, at some temple, a bell rang once, a deep *bong* that rolled out a long, long way through the dusk.

This was Chang'an.

Chang'an, fourteen hundred years ago.

***

The ninth year of Wude, the ninth day of the eighth month.

Shen You was a regular in the history corner of the video sites, and he'd binged at least twenty different takes on the Xuanwu Gate Incident. On the fourth day of the sixth month of the ninth year of Wude, Li Shimin, the Prince of Qin, had ambushed and killed the Crown Prince and the Prince of Qi at the Xuanwu Gate, then forced his father to give up the throne. In the eighth month came the enthronement.

Which was today.

As for the Turks, he remembered that too: only days after Li Shimin took the throne, Jieli Khan would push all the way to the banks of the Wei River, and the new emperor would have to ride out of the city in person to swear a pact.

"Well, hell," he murmured. "Dropped straight into the main event."

By rights, this was where he should panic. No identity, no money, a monk's head of stubble, and clothes from fourteen hundred years in the future.

But he gave some honest thought to what he'd left behind in the modern world: a rented room at 3,200 a month, a product manager who never stopped changing the requirements, and a future in which, at thirty-five, he'd almost certainly get "optimized."

Nothing he absolutely had to go back for.

"Fine." Shen You let out a long breath and, to his own surprise, felt like laughing. "Today's Li Shimin's first day on the job. Mine too. What are the odds."

***

Just then, a line of text floated up in front of his eyes.

The characters were pale gold, translucent, hanging in midair. He turned his head left, and the text went left with it. He shut his eyes, and the text was still there.

[Deathsworn Summoning]

[Day 1]

[Summons Available Today: 1]

Right after came a block of information, as if someone had shoved it straight into his brain:

Refreshes daily at the Hour of the Rat. One summons on the first day, increasing by one each day thereafter. Each day's quota expires if unused.

The deathsworn possess biosynthetic bodies, no different from ordinary people. Each carries one human skill.

The deathsworn are absolutely loyal to the Host.

Shen You stared at the lines for a while. No yelling, no fuss. He just raised an eyebrow.

So on top of the language pack, there was a system.

Okay, the customer support here was genuinely five stars.

"Summon."

The air in front of him warped, like heat shimmering off asphalt at the height of summer, or like someone had taken a knife and slit open an invisible sheet of paper.

A man stepped out through the opening.

About forty, thin, with a tuft of goatee, in a half-worn round-collared robe of coarse cloth. The most striking thing about him was his hands: long, slender fingers, the tips stained with ink that would never wash out.

The man didn't rush to kneel. First he looked at Shen You, then swept a quick glance around: wasteland, burial mounds, setting sun, the ward walls in the distance.

Only then did he lift the hem of his robe and drop to one knee.

"Your servant greets the Master."

The gold text in front of Shen You changed.

[Deathsworn · One]

[Skill: Forgery (Grandmaster)]

[Loyalty: Absolute]

Shen You hadn't missed that once-over. Before kneeling, the man had first worked out where he was.

Interesting.

"Get up and talk. What can you forge?"

"Documents, contracts, seals, handwriting, letters of appointment, travel passes." The man rose, his voice low. "Anything written on paper or carved into a seal, I can make so it's no different from the real thing."

"What about household registration?"

"The registers are kept at the county office. I can make one identical to theirs, but someone has to put it in." He paused. "That can wait. What the Master lacks right now isn't a household registration. It's a travel pass that will stand up to a look, and a *futou*."

"A *futou*?"

The man's gaze settled on Shen You's buzz cut. He said nothing.

Shen You laughed. "You noticed too. The old man who came by just now took me for a monk who'd been thrown out of his temple."

"Then be a monk." The man didn't even need to think. "A monk culled and returned to lay life: short hair, no family property, a past no one can quite account for. In Chang'an these days, that's the most ordinary thing there is. No one will look into it. If I build the papers around that, it's the least trouble."

Shen You studied him, a new gleam of interest in his eyes.

"And money?" he asked, deliberately. "Kaiyuan Tongbao coins. Can you make those?"

"I can. Give me copper, a furnace, and molds."

"So should you?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Privately cast coin is only useful once it's spent. The more you spend, the faster you're exposed. When the authorities go after illicit coin, they never start by looking for who made it. They follow the coins back." The man paused. "If the Master only wants to live it up for three months, make them. If the Master wants to last, don't."

Shen You laughed out loud.

The very first man he'd summoned already had the nerve to tell him no, and the sense to make a solid case for it.

"All right. Then we don't make them."

***

"What's your name?"

"I have no name, Master. I ask that you grant me one."

Shen You thought about it. "You're the first. So, Zhou One."

"Zhou One." The man said it over once and nodded. "Thank you, Master."

To Tang ears, "Zhou One" meant nothing more than the eldest of the Zhou family, about as ordinary as a name got. Only Shen You knew how rotten it really was. Where he came from, *zhou yi* meant Monday, the most miserable day of the week.

That's what he got for being first. Somebody had to be Monday, and Monday was always going to work the hardest.

***

"It'll be dark soon." Zhou One glanced up at the sky. "Once the ward gates close, the ward warden makes his rounds. If he runs into an unfamiliar face, there'll be questions."

"I'm not afraid of questions," Shen You said. "They're just a hassle. Getting grilled like a thief on my first day in Chang'an? Not a great look if word gets around."

"There's a ruined temple to the east."

"That's what the old man said."

"Then it really is empty," Zhou One said. "People around here only point strangers that way because they all know there's no one there."

He went on. "Tonight we make do at the temple. Tomorrow I'll go to the West Market for clothes, a *futou*, paper, ink, and carving knives, then come back and make the travel pass. Only—"

"Only, that takes money."

They exchanged a look.

Shen You started going through his pockets.

Left pants pocket: phone. Battery at 12 percent. No service.

Right pants pocket: half a pack of tissues. A blister strip of mints, four left.

Back pocket: one plastic lighter. Green. One-fifty at the checkout.

Not one thing that counted as money.

He lined the three things up on his palm and cracked himself up. Other people who time-traveled at least showed up with a jade pendant on them. He'd shown up carrying the impulse buys from beside a convenience-store register.

Zhou One's eyes went over the three items one by one and came to rest on the little green thing.

"What is this?"

"It makes fire."

Shen You flicked his thumb. *Click.* A tiny flame stood up in the dusk. He let go, and the flame shrank back and died.

Zhou One took it, held it up close to look, then copied him and rolled his thumb.

The wheel spun with a *skritch*, spat a few sparks, and didn't catch.

He didn't fumble at it again. He raised his eyes to Shen You's hand instead.

"Don't let go once you've rolled it. Keep going and press down on the part underneath." Shen You mimed it. "Roll and press, all in one go."

The second time, the flame leapt up.

Zhou One watched it for the space of a breath, lifted his thumb, and handed the thing back.

"Less trouble than a fire steel."

"Go easy on it." Shen You tucked it back into his back pocket. "Once the little bit of fuel inside burns out, it's just an empty shell."

Zhou One nodded and didn't give it another look.

Which left the money problem right where it was.

"I'll go to the West Market tomorrow regardless," Zhou One said. "Money or no money, I need to go once first."

"Empty-handed?"

"Empty-handed is when you see clearest." He gazed at the western sky, darkening little by little. "The money that changes hands in the West Market in a single day could feed this whole ward for a year. Wherever money piles up, there are always cracks."

"No stealing. No robbing."

"My trade doesn't call for climbing walls."

Shen You didn't press any further.

Before they left, Shen You circled back to the crooked jujube tree and took the badge down. Plastic case, color photo, and the photo was this exact face. This was not something he could leave lying around for someone else to find.

He stuffed it into his pants pocket.

***

Sure enough, the temple was empty.

It had probably been a small temple once. After the culling, it had been stripped down to a single main hall. The altar table had lost a leg and collapsed. Someone had made off with the Buddha's head, leaving only the clay body sitting upright, a patch of starlight falling on it through the roof.

From outside came a long, drawn-out cry: "Clo-o-osing the wa-a-ard ga-a-ates!"

Then the heavy groan of the ward gate, and the night watchmen's footsteps fading away.

With the ward gates shut, all of Chang'an was cut into a hundred-odd sealed-off squares. Nobody in any of them was getting out before dawn.

Autumn insects chirred in a chorus from the grass. Somewhere far off a dog barked twice, then fell quiet.

Zhou One didn't lie down. He dragged a stone over and sat on it by the doorway.

"Not sleeping?"

"The Master sleeps. I keep watch."

Shen You didn't insist. He lay down on a heap of dry straw, hands pillowed behind his head, looking at the stars through the hole in the roof.

He couldn't sleep. Not out of fear. He was doing math.

Occupational hazard of being a programmer.

Day one, one. Day two, two. Day three, three...

N summoned on Day N meant the running total was N times N-plus-one, divided by two.

Day thirty: 465.

Day forty: 820.

He had a vague memory that on the day of Xuanwu Gate, the Prince of Qin's household had eight hundred or so men it could actually put to use, counting every last one.

Forty days from now, he'd have more people than Li Shimin had on the day of Xuanwu Gate.

Day one hundred: 5,050.

One year: 66,795.

Three years: 600,060.

He didn't remember it exactly, but the militia of the entire Great Tang came to six hundred thousand or so, all told.

Three years from now, he alone would have as many people as the whole army of the Great Tang.

And the number would still be climbing every day.

The temple stayed quiet for a long time.

Shen You didn't sit up, and he didn't get excited. He just lay there, hands behind his head, the corners of his mouth curling up a little at a time.

He'd figured something out.

This cheat didn't need him to be especially smart. It didn't need him to bust his ass. It didn't even need him to win.

It only needed him to stay alive.

Play it steady.

As long as he played it steady, didn't do anything stupid, didn't rush, just let the days go by one by one and the numbers grow one by one—

Sooner or later, the whole realm would be his.

As for that chair...

Court at the fifth watch. Memorials that never ended. Wei Zheng chewing him out in front of the entire court, finger jabbing at his nose.

Whoever wanted it could have it.

The realm being his and sitting in that chair were two completely different things.

So what was next?

The stars twinkled in the hole in the roof. The night sky fourteen hundred years ago had no light pollution; even with half a moon still hanging in the west, the stars were packed in ridiculously thick.

Li Shimin, Fang Xuanling, Du Ruhui, Yuchi Gong, Cheng Yaojin, Wei Zheng...

Names he'd seen countless times in textbooks and videos, every one of them alive right now, just to the north, behind those dark, looming palace walls.

Shen You suddenly smiled.

"To play, obviously."

***

"Master." Zhou One's voice came from the doorway. "Tomorrow, it will be two?"

"Two."

"I'm somewhat curious what they'll be able to do."

"Me too." Shen You yawned and rolled over.

That was the whole point of opening a loot box.

***

The ninth year of Wude, the ninth day of the eighth month.

On this day, Li Shimin ascended the throne in Xiande Hall of the Eastern Palace, the realm in his hands.

On this day, Shen You lay in a ruined temple in the south of Chang'an whose Buddha had lost its head, with nothing in his hands but one Zhou One.

But he wasn't in any hurry at all.

Tomorrow would be two. The day after, three.

He had all the time in the world.
