# Setting Notes for New Readers (Chapters 1–19)

*A one-page orientation to the world, the premise and the cast. The story explains itself as it goes; this is for readers who like to know where they're standing. Light spoilers through Chapter 19 only.*

---

## The premise in one paragraph

Shen You, a burned-out programmer who fully expected to be "optimized" out of his job at thirty-five, wakes up on waste ground south of Chang'an, capital of the Tang dynasty, on the evening of the ninth day of the eighth month, 626 CE. He has no money, no papers and no household registration. He also has a system. At the Hour of the Rat each night it lets him summon *deathsworn*: people who are absolutely loyal to him, each with one grandmaster-level skill. On Day 1 he gets one. On Day 2, two. On Day N, N. The running total grows with the square of the days, so the real pressure in this book is never money. It is how fast a household of strangers is growing in a city that counts heads.

Shen You doesn't want the throne. He wants to live well, and to have fun doing it.

## When and where: Chang'an, September 626

- **The week before the story:** Li Shimin ambushed and killed his elder brother, the Crown Prince, and his younger brother, the Prince of Qi, at the Xuanwu Gate. His father, Emperor Gaozu, then stepped aside. Li Shimin took the throne on the same day Shen You wakes up, and proclaimed a general amnesty. Anyone in Chang'an who is clever is busy working out which door to knock on.
- **The mood of the city:** Old loyalists of the dead Crown Prince are still hiding in the capital. The great families are rushing toward the new court. In the north, the Eastern Turks are moving troops, and everyone knows the new emperor's first autumn will be tested. Grain prices are already creeping up.
- **How the city is built:** Chang'an is a grid of about a hundred walled wards. The gates lock at dusk, and nobody is on the avenues at night. Shopping happens only in the East and West Markets, which open and close by drum and gong. Shen You's home ground, Kaiming Ward, is a quiet southern ward half-emptied of people, which is why a haunted house there costs only twenty strings.
- **How people are counted:** Every subject is entered in a household register kept by the county. Travel needs a pass. A man without papers is nobody, and nobody asks twice what became of him. This is why Zhou One, the forger, is the first deathsworn Shen You calls up.
- **Where things stand by Chapter 16:** The new emperor invests his empress on the twenty-first. That same night word comes that the two Turkic khans, Jieli and Tuli, have crossed the border with more than a hundred thousand horsemen, and the capital goes under martial law. Millet that cost thirty coins a dou before martial law tops a hundred. Yuchi Jingde wins a victory at Jingyang, but by the twenty-eighth the khan's host is on the Wei River just north of the city, and the court has to decide what it will pay to send him home.
- **Where things stand by Chapter 19:** The treaty is sealed at the Bian Bridge on the thirtieth with a white horse, and paid for in silk, gold and silver. On the first of the ninth month martial law lifts and the city gates open. That morning Shen You leaves Chang'an in a curtained hat, riding a camel, listed on a forged travel pass as a Sogdian merchant's nephew. A hundred and twenty of his people travel with him, hidden in three merchants' camel trains. They cross the Wei and turn north after horses, behind the Turks on their way home. North of the river they find what the treaty didn't cover: a village burned and nineteen people led off on a rope the afternoon the treaty was made. By that night they are camped in a dry gully, and the tail of the Turkic host is close enough to have riders watching the road.

- **Days and dates:** Day N falls on day N + 8 of the eighth month. Day 1 is the ninth, Day 13 (the empress's investiture) the twenty-first, Day 18 the twenty-sixth. The new day begins at the Hour of the Rat, so the summons that comes late on the night of the twenty-eighth counts toward Day 21. Chapter 16 ends on Day 22, the thirtieth, with 253 people.
- **Into the ninth month:** the eighth month has thirty days, so Day 23 is the first of the ninth month. Chapters 17 to 19 all happen on that one day, with **276** people, two of whom are away on the road to Yizhou.
- **Within ten paces:** each night's new people appear within ten paces of Shen You, wherever he is. If he is on the road, so are they.
- Each deathsworn has one skill at grandmaster level, and the roll is random. The first twenty-eight: forgery, gambling, Sichuan cooking, hand-to-hand combat, carpentry, medicine, vocal mimicry, interpreting, masonry, accounting, tailoring, agriculture, mounted archery, perfumery, smithing, brewing, veterinary medicine, storytelling, tracking, crossbow mechanics, square dancing, sugar refining, pastry, pottery, singing, blade arts, climbing and beekeeping. Since then: battle formations, mathematics, pickling, cartwrighting, ice cellaring, hawk training, ferrying, polo, reading the stars, raising camels and more. On Day 23: cooking for an army in the field, saddlery, ear cleaning, oil pressing, pot mending, well digging and others.
- They are absolutely loyal, but they are not yes-men. They argue, joke and push back, and the cooks and carpenters are as proud of their work as the fighters.
- **Names:** a surname plus a number, seven to a surname: Zhou One to Zhou Seven, then Zheng, Feng, Chen and so on. To the Tang, these sound like ordinary birth-order names. To a modern Chinese ear, Zhou One through Seven are Monday through Sunday.
- **Scattered by design:** Shen You sends his people out to run shops, workshops and errands all over the city. A watcher can count his household's rice and carts, but never his heads.

## The black lacquer box

It is hidden in the beams of the house Shen You buys in Chapter 5. It bears the private seal of a man on the dead Crown Prince's staff, and Qingluo recognizes the style. Wei San says it holds a roster the Crown Prince left behind; nobody else who wants it will say. Shen You won't open it. A note pinned to his door gives him ten days to hand it over, and he answers that in ten days he will have a hundred and twenty people. The deadline falls due in Chapter 12. The box stays with Shen You.

## A few things the book doesn't stop to explain

- **Money:** one coin is one bronze *Kaiyuan Tongbao*. One string is a thousand coins. A sesame flatbread costs eight to ten coins. A haunted house costs twenty strings. A single lighter, sold as a wonder from the far west, fetches one gold ingot plus two strings. Bolts of silk also serve as money, and gold and silver are weighed in taels.
- **Grain:** sold by the *dou*. Ten *sheng* make a *dou*, and ten *dou* make a *shi*. A person eats about two *sheng* a day.
- **Time:** the Hour of the Rat is 11 p.m. to 1 a.m., and the Tang counted it as the start of a new day. It is also the third watch of the night. Midday is the Hour of the Horse. A double-hour is two hours.
- **Titles:** deathsworn and servants call Shen You "Master". Strangers say "young master". Someone who has learned his name says "Young Master Shen". A "Little Master" is a young monk. A "manager" runs a business for its owner; a "steward" runs a household. In the guard armies, a Colonel serves under a Senior Colonel, who serves under the Generals. A County Commandant handles a county's arrests and criminal cases.
- **Slavery:** the people sold at the Human and Horse Market were legally property, as in every great household of the age. Shen You pays what he must, treats them as people, and fixes house rules the next day: no kneeling, shared meals, wages, one day of rest in ten.
- **The Turks** are the Eastern Turkic Khaganate, steppe nomads north of China. They are not modern Turkey. Jieli is their supreme khan; Tuli, "the lesser khan", rules beside him and camps apart. On the way home they live off the land, which Ashina calls "beating the grass for grain."
- **Hats:** Shen You travels in a *curtained hat*, with gauze hanging from the brim to his shoulders. Twelfth Daughter wears a *veiled hat*, the older style. The English keeps the two apart.
- **Leaving the capital:** since Chapter 10 the county has forbidden Shen You to leave the capital while his papers are checked. In his own household's rule, crossing the Wei counts as leaving, so in Chapter 18 he pays himself a one-coin fine into the river.
