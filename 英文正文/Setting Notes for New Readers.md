# Setting Notes for New Readers (Chapters 1–9)

*A one-page orientation to the world, the premise and the cast. The story explains itself as it goes; this is for readers who like to know where they're standing. Light spoilers through Chapter 9 only.*

---

## The premise in one paragraph

Shen You, a laid-off programmer, wakes up on waste ground south of Chang'an, capital of the Tang dynasty, on the evening of the ninth day of the eighth month, 626 CE. He has no money, no papers and no household registration. He also has a system. At the Hour of the Rat each night it lets him summon *deathsworn*: people who are absolutely loyal to him, each with one grandmaster-level skill. On Day 1 he gets one. On Day 2, two. On Day N, N. The running total grows with the square of the days, so the real pressure in this book is never money. It is how fast a household of strangers is growing in a city that counts heads.

Shen You doesn't want the throne. He wants to live well, and to have fun doing it.

## When and where: Chang'an, September 626

- **The week before the story:** Li Shimin ambushed and killed his elder brother, the Crown Prince, and his younger brother, the Prince of Qi, at the Xuanwu Gate. His father, Emperor Gaozu, then stepped aside. Li Shimin took the throne on the same day Shen You wakes up. Anyone in Chang'an who is clever is busy working out which door to knock on.
- **The mood of the city:** Old loyalists of the dead Crown Prince are still hiding in the capital. The great families are rushing toward the new court. In the north, the Eastern Turks are moving troops, and everyone knows the new emperor's first autumn will be tested. Grain prices are already creeping up.
- **How the city is built:** Chang'an is a grid of about a hundred walled wards. The gates lock at dusk, and nobody is on the avenues at night. Shopping happens only in the East and West Markets, which open and close by drum and gong. Shen You's home ground, Kaiming Ward, is a quiet southern ward half-emptied of people, which is why a haunted house there costs only twenty strings.
- **How people are counted:** Every subject is entered in a household register kept by the county. Travel needs a pass. A man without papers is nobody, and nobody asks twice what became of him. This is why Zhou One, the forger, is the first deathsworn Shen You calls up.

## How the system works

| Day | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Summoned that night | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
| Running total | 1 | 3 | 6 | 10 | 15 | 21 | 28 | 36 | 45 |

- Each deathsworn has one skill at grandmaster level, and the roll is random. So far: forgery, gambling, Sichuan cooking, fighting, carpentry, medicine, ventriloquism, translation, masonry, accounting, tailoring, agronomy, mounted archery, perfumery, blacksmithing, brewing, veterinary work, storytelling, tracking, crossbow-making, square dancing, sugar-making, pastry, pottery, singing, swordsmanship, climbing and beekeeping.
- They are absolutely loyal, but they are not yes-men. They argue, joke and push back, and the cooks and carpenters are as proud of their work as the fighters.
- **Names:** a surname plus a number, seven to a surname: Zhou One to Zhou Seven, then Zheng, Chen, Feng and so on. To the Tang, these sound like ordinary birth-order names. To a modern Chinese ear, Zhou One through Seven are Monday through Sunday.
- **Scattered by design:** Shen You sends his people out to run shops, workshops and errands all over the city. A watcher can count his household's rice and carts, but never his heads.

## The black lacquer box

It is hidden in the beams of the house Shen You buys in Chapter 5. It bears the private seal of a man on the dead Crown Prince's staff, and Qingluo recognizes the style. Nobody who wants it will say what is in it. Shen You won't open it. A note pinned to his door gives him ten days to hand it over, and he answers that in ten days he will have a hundred and twenty people.

## A few things the book doesn't stop to explain

- **Money:** one coin is one bronze *Kaiyuan Tongbao*. One string is a thousand coins. A sesame flatbread costs eight to ten coins. A haunted house costs twenty strings. A single lighter, sold as a wonder from the far west, fetches one gold ingot plus two strings.
- **Time:** the Hour of the Rat is 11 p.m. to 1 a.m., and the Tang counted it as the start of a new day. It is also the third watch of the night. Midday is the Hour of the Horse. A double-hour is two hours.
- **Titles:** deathsworn and servants call Shen You "Master". Strangers say "young master". Someone who has learned his name says "Young Master Shen". A "Little Master" is a young monk. A "manager" runs a business for its owner; a "steward" runs a household.
- **Slavery:** the people sold at the Human and Horse Market were legally property, as in every great household of the age. Shen You pays what he must, treats them as people, and fixes house rules the next day: no kneeling, shared meals, wages, one day of rest in ten.
- **The Turks** are the Eastern Turkic Khaganate, steppe nomads north of China. They are not modern Turkey.
