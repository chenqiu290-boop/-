# Glossary (Chapters 1–4)

*For readers who want the background. Nothing here is needed to follow the story.*

## The system

- **Deathsworn** (singular and plural): the absolutely loyal people the Host summons, each with one grandmaster-level skill.
- **Summons**: on Day N, N deathsworn appear. The running total after N days is N(N+1)/2. Unused summons expire at the end of the day.

| Day | 1 | 3 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 100 | 365 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total | 1 | 6 | 55 | 210 | 465 | 820 | 5,050 | 66,795 |

- **Naming**: deathsworn take a surname from the *Hundred Family Surnames* plus a number, seven to a surname. To Tang ears these sound like ordinary birth-order names. To a modern Chinese ear, Zhou One through Zhou Seven are Monday through Sunday.
- **Sanniang**: "Third Daughter." A common way to address a woman who is third in her family.

## Time

- **Wude** (618–626): the reign era of the Tang's first emperor. The ninth year of Wude is 626 CE. By the Western calendar, the ninth day of the eighth month falls in early September. The next era, **Zhenguan**, begins in 627.
- **Double-hour**: a two-hour unit. **The Hour of the Rat** is 11 p.m. to 1 a.m., and in the old reckoning it is where the new day begins. It is also the **third watch**. **The Hour of the Horse** is 11 a.m. to 1 p.m.
- **Watches**: the night is divided into five. The **fifth watch** is roughly 3 to 5 a.m., when court convenes.
- **The twelve double-hours**: each is named for an animal of the zodiac.

| Rat | Ox | Tiger | Rabbit | Dragon | Snake |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 p.m.–1 a.m. | 1–3 a.m. | 3–5 a.m. | 5–7 a.m. | 7–9 a.m. | 9–11 a.m. |

| Horse | Goat | Monkey | Rooster | Dog | Pig |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 11 a.m.–1 p.m. | 1–3 p.m. | 3–5 p.m. | 5–7 p.m. | 7–9 p.m. | 9–11 p.m. |

## Money

- **Coin**: one bronze Kaiyuan Tongbao coin, first cast in 621. The name means "inaugurating a new era" and has nothing to do with the later Kaiyuan reign.
- **String**: 1,000 coins on a cord. For scale: a sesame flatbread costs 8 to 10 coins, and a fire steel costs 1.
- **Bolt of silk**: used as money.

## Chang'an

- **Ward** (*fang*): a walled residential block whose gates are locked from dusk to dawn. Chang'an had 108 of them. Being on the avenues after the gates close breaks curfew. The **ward warden** is the official who holds the gate keys.
- **East Market and West Market**: the only two commercial districts in the city. The West Market opens at the Hour of the Horse.
- **Imperial City and Palace City**: the government offices, and the palace itself, in the north of the city.
- **Eastern Palace**: the crown prince's residence. After the Incident, Li Shimin was named crown prince and took the throne there. His father, now the **Retired Emperor** (Li Yuan, Emperor Gaozu), stayed in **Taiji Palace**.
- **County**: Chang'an was split into two counties along Vermilion Bird Avenue. Kaiming Ward belongs to Wannian County. **Constables** are the county's detectives and enforcers. **Patrolmen** guard the streets and gates.
- **Human and Horse Market** (*kouma hang*): the row in the West Market where people and horses were sold. *Kou*, "mouths," was the official word for heads of people. Under Tang law, slaves were a base class and counted as their master's property. They could be bought, sold, given away or inherited. Every sale had to be recorded in a deed drawn up at the Market Office, and a master could free a slave with a written release.

## History

- **Xuanwu Gate Incident** (summer 626): Li Shimin ambushed and killed his elder brother, the Crown Prince, and his younger brother, the Prince of Qi, then forced his father to step aside. He took the throne on the ninth day of the eighth month.
- **The Turks**: the Eastern Turkic Khaganate, steppe nomads north of China. Not modern Turkey. **Jieli Khan** is their supreme ruler.
- **The culling of monks and nuns**: a decree of spring 626 sending most monks and nuns back to lay life and tearing down most temples. It was rescinded after the Incident.
- **Militia** (*fubing*): registered farmer-soldiers who served in rotation, the backbone of the early Tang army.
- **Wei Zheng**: the minister famous for scolding the emperor to his face.
- **Zhangsun family**: the clan of Li Shimin's wife and her brother Zhangsun Wuji, one of the Incident's key plotters.
- **Dou family**: Li Shimin's mother's clan.
- **Early Tang wars**: Taiyuan (617), where the Li family rose against the Sui; Huoyi (617), where Song Laosheng, a Sui general, was beaten; Qianshuiyuan (618), Li Shimin's one great defeat.
- **Yuchi Jingde** (also called Yuchi Gong) and **Cheng Yaojin** (also called Cheng Zhijie) are each one person.

## Offices and ranks

- **Colonel** (*langjiang*) and **Senior Colonel** (*zhonglangjiang*): officers in one of the guard armies stationed in the capital. A colonel (upper fifth rank) serves under a senior colonel (lower fourth rank). Above both sit the guard's generals and its grand general.
- **Crown Prince's Librarian** (*taizi xima*): an official of the Eastern Palace in charge of the crown prince's books and records, lower fifth rank. The title reads literally as "horse washer," but it comes from an older word for the outriders who went ahead of the prince's horse.

## People and peoples

- **Hu**: the Tang's catch-all term for Central Asian and other western foreigners. **Sogdians** were the great merchant people of the Silk Road, around Samarkand.
- **Fulin**: the Tang name for the Eastern Roman (Byzantine) Empire. Its 24-letter alphabet is Greek.

## Forms of address

- **Master / the Master**: how deathsworn and servants address Shen You. Said in the third person, it is respect rather than distance.
- **young master**: lowercase, a stranger's polite term for a well-dressed young man.
- **Young Master Shen**: used once the speaker knows his surname.
- **Little Master**: a polite address to a young monk.
- **Elder**: a respectful address to an old man.
- **Manager** (*guanshi*): runs a business for its owner. A **steward** runs a household. They are different jobs.
- **Dou the Eleventh**: Tang men were often named by surname plus birth order, counted across all male cousins of a generation. "Dou the Eleventh" is the eleventh son of the clan's generation. A bare surname-plus-number such as **Fan Da** or **Wang Da** is a plain name.

## Things

- **Futou**: the soft black cloth headwrap with two trailing tails that Tang men wore.
- **Travel pass** (*guosuo*): the document needed to travel between districts. **Household register**: every subject was entered in the county registers. Someone with no entry had no standing.
- **Chupu**: a dice-and-pieces gambling game. Five pieces are thrown. All five black is *lu*, the best throw. *Zhi* is the second best.
- **Nine-Two, Nine-Three, Nine-Five**: positions in the *Book of Changes*. Nine-Five is the sovereign's position. The Sui planners left that ridge to temples so no subject would live "above" the emperor.
- **Crimson and green robes**: official ranks. Crimson was for middle and upper ranks, green for lower.
- **Halberds arrayed before a gate**: a privilege of officials of the third rank and above.
