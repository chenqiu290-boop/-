# Chapter 7: The Human and Horse Market

The Hour of the Rat. The sixth day.

Six people.

[Deathsworn · Sixteen]

[Skill: Brewing (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Seventeen]

[Skill: Veterinary Medicine (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Eighteen]

[Skill: Storytelling (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Nineteen]

[Skill: Tracking (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Twenty]

[Skill: Crossbow Mechanics (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Twenty-One]

[Skill: Square Dancing (Grandmaster)]

When that last skill popped up, Shen You stared at it for a good long while.

She was a round-faced woman in her early forties with a bright, carrying voice. The first thing she did on getting to her feet was clap her hands and sweep a look over the dark crowd filling the courtyard. "All these people—first thing in the morning, everybody's getting up and loosening those joints together. Every last one of you standing there like a fence post. What good is that?"

Shen You pictured it: twenty-one people in a courtyard in the Tang dynasty, wiggling along to her count.

He couldn't help it. He laughed out loud.

Once he was done laughing, it stopped seeming so funny. Getting twenty or thirty people to move to one beat, one step, in perfect unison—anywhere else, that skill had a name. Drilling troops.

The tracker was a lanky, long-faced man. Zhou Four took him to see the spot where last night's intruder had gone over the wall. He crouched at the foot of the wall and studied it for a long time, then climbed to the top and studied it for a long time more. Without a word, he dropped down the other side and was gone.

Feng Two through Feng Seven. Twenty-one people.

***

At daybreak, Zheng Three gave her report.

"Zhou Two won back another eighteen strings yesterday. That makes twenty-three strings and four hundred coins in the house. Qian Biao's pile of stolen goods is separate. It's not on the books."

"Leave that pile alone," Shen You said. "That's Qian Biao's life right there."

Zhou One stood to one side, frowning. "Master, the man last night left word: within ten days. For the next few days, it would be best if the Master went out less."

"Life goes on." Shen You got up and stretched. "He said ten days, so it's ten days. If he really wanted to make his move today, he wouldn't have stopped at pinning a knife to the gate last night."

He thought for a moment and added, "The Eastern Palace fell two months ago. Its old people are scattered to the four winds. Ten days is probably how long he needs to round enough of them up."

Zhou Two had gone out first thing, saying he wanted to look over some new gambling houses by the East Market.

Shen You picked a few people to come along: Zheng Three to handle the money, Zhou Four for protection, Zheng One because he spoke the foreign tongues, and Zhou Six to check whether the people for sale had anything wrong with them.

***

The Human and Horse Market lay in the northwest corner of the West Market, hard by the horse traders' row.

They smelled it before they reached it: horse dung, hay, the rank musk of livestock, all mixed with the dusty smell of earth baking in the sun. A row of wooden rails had dozens of horses tied along it—tall Hexi horses, short, stocky Turkic horses—and a few Hu merchants were walking mounts back and forth across the yard, hooves churning the yellow dirt to mush. Buyers pried open the horses' mouths to check their teeth, slapped their rumps, and haggled with the brokers.

Past the horse traders, farther in, stood a row of wooden sheds with thatched roofs. Under them, standing or sitting in a long line, were several dozen people: Guanzhong folk in ragged short tunics of coarse cloth; Liao tribespeople from Lingnan, dark, thin, and small, speaking a tongue nobody could follow; Hu brought all the way from the west by Hu merchants. Some kept their heads down and never made a sound. Some watched every buyer who walked past with desperate, hungry eyes.

A broker in his forties came out to meet them. His name was Sun. He carried a tattered palm-leaf fan, and when he smiled he showed a mouthful of yellow teeth.

"Buying people, young master? Someone to see to your daily needs? Then you'll want to look at the top grade. This way, please."

***

At the very back of the shed stood seven or eight young women.

Sun the broker reached straight for the nearest one to pry her mouth open and show Shen You her teeth.

"Don't," Shen You said. "I'm not buying a horse."

He tipped his head at Zhou Six. Zhou Six stepped forward and went down the line one by one, looking at their complexions, the whites of their eyes, their fingernails, now and then laying two fingers on a wrist. Sun the broker stood by gaping. Twenty years he'd been selling people, and this was the first time he'd seen a buyer bring a woman physician along to pick out maids.

When Zhou Six was done, she came back and murmured a few words. Shen You nodded and said to the row of women, "Tell me what you can do. In your own words."

The one at the far end spoke first.

"This maid can brew tea, dress hair, burn incense, and grind ink. I can read, and I write a neat hand in small regular script."

Her voice wasn't loud, but every word was crisp, shaped by the accent of the capital's official speech.

She was eighteen or nineteen, tall and slender, a good half a head taller than the girls beside her. An oval face, fair and clean-skinned, with long, fine brows that looked like a single light stroke of pale ink. Her eyes weren't large, but they were very bright. She kept them slightly lowered, except for the moment Shen You came close—then they flicked to his hands, and then to his face.

Her hair was done up in a simple single knot, held with a plain wooden pin. She wore a blue-green jacket and skirt, washed until the color had faded, with a fine line of darning along the hem, yet there wasn't a single wrinkle anywhere in it. Her hands were folded in front of her. She stood perfectly straight, shoulders and back unbowed, chin neither lifted nor dropped.

Of everyone in that shed, she was the only one who looked like she was standing on the veranda of a great house, and not in a livestock pen.

"What's your name?"

"My former household called this maid Qingluo."

Sun the broker held his fan up in front of his mouth and leaned in. "Young master, I won't lie to you. This one came out of the Prince of Qi's household. After what happened in the sixth month, she was confiscated by the state, and a few days ago the Court of the National Granaries let a batch of them go for sale. Her manners are the very best. It's just her background... buyers all say it's bad luck."

Qingluo raised her eyes and looked at Shen You.

"What would the young master be buying this maid for?"

Sun the broker's face darkened. "Insolent—"

"Let her ask." Shen You gave it real thought. "Brewing tea. Doing my hair. Keeping me company in conversation. And the great families of Chang'an—who's who, who's friendly with whom, who's got a grudge against whom—I'd like you to help me sort them out."

Qingluo looked at him for a long time. Then she lowered her eyes again.

"How much?"

"Fifteen strings."

"She's been standing in your shed seven or eight days now, hasn't she?" Zheng Three stepped forward. "Ten strings."

Before Sun the broker could open his mouth, Zheng Three had already counted out two strings and stuffed them into his arms. "The deposit. The rest when the deed's drawn up."

Sun the broker hugged the two strings to his chest and gave a wry smile. "...Fine. Ten strings."

***

The deposit had barely landed in Sun the broker's arms when someone came over from the horse traders' side.

The first in were two household slaves in clothes of fine hemp, belted with leather to match. One look, and anyone could tell they came from a great house. They stepped apart to either side, and only then did a young man walk in between them.

He was twenty-five or twenty-six, pale and beardless, in a round-collared robe of sky blue. The fabric was nothing showy, but the cuffs were embroidered with a subtle pattern. A small jade fish hung at his waist. He didn't walk quickly, but his stride was steady, every step as if measured out in advance. Behind him came a tall Hu slave with curly hair and deep-set eyes, a black-lacquered horn bow slung across his back. He didn't say a word.

Sun the broker's face changed. He snapped his fan shut and hurried forward, bent at the waist. "Young Master Pei! What brings you here in person..."

The young man waved this away. His gaze swept the shed and came to rest on Qingluo.

"Is this her?"

"Yes, yes..." Sun the broker wiped away a bead of sweat. "But this young master here has just put down a deposit..."

Only then did the young man look at Shen You. He cupped his hands in salute, his manners faultless. "Pei Shisan, at your service. And how might I address you, young master?"

"Shen You, of Kaiming Ward."

"Young Master Shen." Pei Shisan nodded, his tone perfectly courteous. "The Pei family had meant to buy this maid. Some family business held us up these past few days. I did not expect to arrive a step too late today."

He gestured to the slave beside him. The slave stepped forward and presented a purse to Sun the broker with both hands.

"Twenty strings," Pei Shisan said. "Sun the broker will return Young Master Shen's deposit in full, and the Pei family will add two strings for Young Master Shen by way of apology."

Sun the broker held the purse in both hands, looked at Pei Shisan, then at Shen You. Sweat rolled down his forehead, one bead after another.

Zheng Three took a step forward. Her voice wasn't loud, but every word came through clearly. "You've already taken the deposit, broker. Selling the same girl twice—if the Market Office hears about it, do you still expect to make your living in the West Market?"

Sun the broker's hand jerked. He nearly dropped the purse in the dirt.

Pei Shisan looked at Zheng Three. Rather than taking offense, he smiled.

"The lady is quite right. The market has its rules, and the Pei family does not break the rules."

He turned to Shen You.

"Then I will not buy from Sun the broker. I will buy from Young Master Shen."

"Thirty strings," he said. "She goes to the Pei family. You make twenty strings for nothing, Young Master Shen, and today's trip won't have been wasted."

***

A ring of onlookers had gradually gathered. Hu merchants from the horse traders' row, brokers from the Human and Horse Market, a few men who'd come to buy horses—all of them were looking this way.

"The Pei family's people..."

"Which Pei family?"

"Which do you think? Chancellor Pei's."

"Thirty strings for a maid? One out of the Prince of Qi's household? You couldn't give one of those away..."

Shen You didn't respond.

He wasn't watching Pei Shisan. He was watching Qingluo.

The moment Pei Shisan walked in, Qingluo's face had gone white. Not the white of someone afraid of a particular person. The white of someone who has suddenly understood something and doesn't want to believe it. She stood there without moving a muscle, her fingers clenched tight inside her sleeves.

Thirty strings. For a maid out of the Prince of Qi's household, one every buyer said was bad luck, the Pei family was offering thirty strings.

And they'd "meant to buy her" all along.

Somewhere in Shen You's mind, a string gave a faint twang.

Just then, someone gave his sleeve a light tug from behind.

He turned his head slightly. It was a round-faced girl at the edge of the shed, in a patched jacket and skirt of coarse hemp, mixed in among a few maids meant for rough work. She didn't look at him. Her eyes were on the ground, and her lips barely moved. Her voice was so soft only he could hear it:

"Don't sell, young master. That's the man who runs the Pei family's business. Their people have come three times this month, and they only buy the ones who came out of studies and counting rooms. Of all the people they've bought, I've never seen a single one in the West Market again."

Then she shrank back into the crowd as if she'd never said a word.

Shen You didn't look back. He turned to face Pei Shisan.

"Young Master Pei," he said, "is the Pei family short of maids?"

"No."

"Then why does it have to be her?"

Pei Shisan looked at him, and his smile cooled by a degree.

"You are new to Chang'an, Young Master Shen," he said slowly. "Some things are better left unknown."

"I'm the curious type."

"Curiosity is a fine thing," Pei Shisan said. "But this maid served for more than a year in the study of the Prince of Qi's household. As to what passed in and out of that study—I imagine Young Master Shen can guess."

He paused and lowered his voice a little, so that only the few people closest could hear.

"Since the sixth month, a great many people in Chang'an have not been sleeping well. All of them want to know who now holds the people who came out of the Prince of Qi's study, and how tightly those people keep their mouths shut."

"And the Pei family is one of them?"

Pei Shisan didn't answer. He only looked at him.

That was answer enough.

The remark from the crowd ran through Shen You's head again. Chancellor Pei—the Retired Emperor's number-one man. The new emperor had been on the throne only a few days, and anyone could see that the Pei threshold was about to go cold.

A family on its way down feared nothing so much as old accounts being dug up. So it bought up the people who knew those old accounts. And once it had bought them—

He remembered what the round-faced girl had said. Never seen a single one again.

Shen You ran the numbers in his head.

One: for a maid every buyer said was bad luck, the Pei family had opened at thirty strings. Whatever she carried was worth a great deal more than thirty strings.

Two: he'd just pulled an Eastern Palace lacquer box out of his own rafters, and some "noble patron" from the Eastern Palace was holding a knife to him with a ten-day deadline. He was groping in the dark. He didn't even know who he was up against. The woman in front of him had spent more than a year in the Prince of Qi's study reading the Eastern Palace's letters. She might be the one person in all of Chang'an best able to help him see this board clearly.

Three: if she was sold to the Pei family, odds were she'd simply be gone.

Money, Zhou Two could win, and there'd be more to earn down the road. This person—miss her today, and she could never be found again.

"Thirty strings," Shen You said. "Not selling."

Pei Shisan's brow twitched.

"Forty strings."

"Not selling."

"Young Master Shen," Pei Shisan said, his voice as courteous as ever, "forty strings would buy four maids like her."

"If you're willing to pay forty strings for her, Young Master Pei," Shen You said with a smile, "then she's worth more than forty. When I do business, I never sell cheap."

He paused, then added, "Besides, she'll be brewing my tea."

Someone in the crowd let out a snort of laughter, then hurriedly clapped a hand over his mouth.

Pei Shisan didn't laugh.

He looked at Shen You for a long time, then glanced at Qingluo. And then he put away the last trace of the smile on his face.

"Then I will not trouble Young Master Shen further."

He cupped his hands in salute, his manners as complete as before. He turned to go, took two steps, then stopped and turned half his face back.

"Young Master Shen, the Pei family will not fight you for her in the West Market. But the Pei family is not the only one in Chang'an with an eye on her."

"Some families don't play by the rules."

***

Pei Shisan left. The Hu slave with the bow brought up the rear. As he passed Zhou Four, his step hitched, and he looked at Zhou Four.

It was a very brief look, like two wolves brushing past each other in the forest, taking each other's scent.

Then he lowered his head and followed his master out.

Zhou Four spoke in Shen You's ear. "That Hu has seen real battle."

"Compared to you?"

Zhou Four thought about it. "On horseback, I'm not his match. On the ground, he's not mine."

***

The crowd broke up. Sun the broker still had the two strings of deposit clutched in his arms and the sweat not yet dry on his face, but the way he looked at Shen You had changed completely.

"That's... that's some nerve you've got, young master."

Shen You ignored him and walked over to Qingluo.

She was still standing where she'd been. Her head was down, but this time Shen You could see it clearly: the rims of her eyes were red.

"You know why they want you?"

Qingluo was silent for a moment, then gave a small nod.

"Then you know that following me might not be a quiet life from here on."

She nodded again.

"So are you coming or not?"

Qingluo raised her head and looked at him. Those eyes that had stayed lowered all this time didn't drop again.

"Just now, Master," she said, her voice a little hoarse, "you wouldn't sell, not even for forty strings."

"Mm."

"Why?"

Shen You thought about it and didn't fob her off.

"There's something in my house that has to do with the Eastern Palace. Somebody wants it, and they've given me ten days," he said. "And I happen to be short one person who can make sense of old Eastern Palace business."

Qingluo's lashes trembled.

"Also," Shen You said, "if I sold you to him, odds are you'd simply be gone. Forty strings for a life—that's a deal I don't make."

Qingluo looked at him and didn't speak for a long time.

"Besides," he said, smiling again, "I really do want to drink the tea you brew."

***

The other two were bought quickly.

One was a Hu girl from Mi, named A'na.

She was even taller than Qingluo. Her chestnut curls were plaited into dozens of thin little braids, gathered at the back of her head with a faded red cord. She had a high-bridged nose and slightly deep-set eyes, amber eyes that showed a hint of gold in the sunlight, with long, thick lashes. Her skin was a light honey brown, with a few faint freckles across her cheeks. She wore an old Hu robe with turned-down lapels, a cloth sash at her waist, and a string of faded five-colored silk threads wound around her wrist.

Zheng One said a single sentence to her in Sogdian. She froze for a moment, and then her eyes went red all at once, and words came tumbling out of her like beans from a split sack, crying and laughing at the same time. Her father had been a merchant and died on the road to Chang'an. The Hu merchants traveling with them had sold her, and for the better part of a year she hadn't heard a word of her own language.

The Hu merchant had kept her pipa back to sell separately. Shen You had it bought back along with her.

Bent neck, four strings, the lacquer on its pear-wood soundboard mostly worn away. A'na sat down with the pipa on an upturned wooden bucket, gave the pegs a twist, and swept the plectrum across the strings.

The western melody came out quick and bright, like camel bells, like wind blowing across sand, each ringing *zheng* climbing higher than the last. Over in the horse traders' row, the Hu merchants haggling over prices shut their mouths and craned to look. When the tune was done, someone tossed a few coins at her feet. A'na hugged the pipa, tilted her head at Shen You, and smiled until her eyes curved into two crescent moons.

The other one jumped out on her own.

"Over here, young master! Buy this maid!"

A round-faced girl squeezed out of the crowd, pushed her way right up to Shen You, made a folded-hands salute, and lifted her face to him without a trace of shyness.

She was eighteen, not tall, sturdily built, her round face tanned the color of wheat. When she smiled, two dimples showed. Her brows were thick, her eyes black and bright. Her hair was done in two little topknots tied with strips of blue cloth. She wore a patched jacket and skirt of coarse hemp, washed spotlessly clean. The backs of her hands bore a few faint scars left by chilblains.

"This maid is called A'xing! I was house-born to the Li family of Pingkang Ward. The family split the household, couldn't divide things evenly, and sold me. Washing, cooking, rolling out soup noodles, feeding chickens, mending—I can do it all, and I don't eat much!"

Shen You looked at her. "The one passing me words behind my back just now. That was you?"

A'xing blinked and didn't deny it. "I've stood in this shed eight days, and the brokers never bother lowering their voices around us. That Young Master Pei is Chancellor Pei's grandnephew. He's been running the Pei family's business these past two years."

"You're not afraid the Pei family will remember you?"

"I'm afraid," A'xing said. "But I was more afraid you'd sell that big sister." She stole a glance at Qingluo, then looked back at Shen You. "I'm not afraid of work. I'm just afraid of following the wrong person."

"Bought."

***

The deeds were drawn up at the Market Office—three deeds. Qingluo, ten strings. A'na, eight strings. The pipa, one string. A'xing, four strings.

When Zheng Three had paid, she put the purse away. "Four hundred coins left. Zhou Two went over to the East Market today. There'll be money coming in tonight."

***

On the way back from the West Market to Kaiming Ward, Shen You walked in front with the three girls behind him. When they reached Vermilion Bird Avenue, he stopped, turned around, and went over the household rules:

No kneeling. A folded-hands salute will do. Whatever the household eats, you eat. Three hundred coins a month each. One day off in every ten. No beatings, no scolding.

A'xing's mouth hung open. It was a long moment before she managed, "Master... you're joking, right?"

A'na tugged at Zheng One's sleeve until she'd heard the whole translation, then burst out with a "*Ha!*" and laughed so hard the pipa nearly slid out of her arms.

Qingluo said nothing. She only watched Shen You's back, for a long time.

***

By the time they got home, the sun had slid well into the west.

The three girls stood in the front courtyard, and for a moment none of them spoke. The fresh arrow hole was still there in the gatepost, and beside it the gash the short knife had left when it was pulled out. People came and went all over the courtyard. From the back courtyard came the clang of Feng One's forge. Zhou Five was planing wood under the eaves, shavings scattered all over the ground.

"Master, the house... has this many people?" A'xing swallowed.

"Twenty-one," Shen You said. "Tomorrow, twenty-eight."

A'xing didn't understand, and didn't dare ask.

As dusk fell, Qingluo set up a small brazier on the veranda of the main hall and brewed a pot of tea. She toasted the tea cake, ground it into powder, waited for the water in the kettle to rise in crab-eye bubbles, sprinkled in salt, and added the tea. Then she took a small piece of ginger from her sleeve and was about to drop it in.

"No ginger. No salt either," Shen You said, leaning back in his chair. "Just plain tea."

Qingluo's hands paused. She didn't ask why. She poured out that kettle and brewed a fresh bowl, plain, and brought it to him in both hands.

The tea was a little astringent, but the aftertaste was sweet, carrying the toasty fragrance of the roasted tea cake.

"That's good."

Over by the well in the back courtyard, A'na sat with her pipa, playing a very slow tune, one note falling after another, like someone thinking about a person far away. A'xing came over with a plate of sesame flatbreads fresh out of the oven, juggling it from her left hand to her right because it was so hot. "Master, Sanniang says we eat three meals a day here! Three! In ten-odd years at the Li house, I only ever got two..."

Qingluo stood behind him, looking at the boxwood comb in her hand, then at his crew cut.

"Master, this hair of yours... this maid can't put it up."

Shen You rubbed his head and laughed out loud.

Qingluo pressed her lips together and smiled too. It was the first time she'd smiled all day.

She put the comb away, laid both hands lightly on his shoulders, and began to knead, neither too hard nor too soft. Wherever she pressed, the tightness let go.

Shen You's eyes drifted half shut.

A hot bath, a chair with a backrest, plain tea, a pipa, someone rubbing his shoulders, someone handing him flatbread while trying not to burn her fingers.

Out there, someone wanted his box, someone wanted his maid, and someone was counting the people in his house.

Six days in the Tang, and life was getting downright lively.

Zhou One came out of the wing room carrying the black lacquer box, meaning to put it away somewhere else, and walked along the veranda.

Qingluo's hands paused on Shen You's shoulders. Only for an instant. Then they went on kneading, with exactly the same pressure as before.

***

The night grew late.

Feng Five, the tracker, came back, soaked with dew.

"The man went over the wall and headed north. Crossed four wards, then switched to a horse on Vermilion Bird Avenue," he said. "The hoofprints went north, into one of the wards just south of the Imperial City. Everyone living around there is an official family. Hard to follow."

Shen You nodded and sent him off to rest.

He was about to get up and go to his room when, from the shadows of the veranda, someone softly called out.

"Master."

It was Qingluo. She stood beside a pillar holding a lamp. There was no telling how long she'd been standing there.

"Today the Master told this maid the truth," she said quietly. "So this maid will tell the Master the truth too. What Young Master Pei said is true."

"What passed in and out of the Prince of Qi's study were letters between the Eastern Palace, the Prince of Qi's household, and the outside world. Who sent gifts to the Prince of Qi, who passed word to the Crown Prince's household, who placed bets on both sides before the Xuanwu Gate..." She paused. "I ground the ink, received the letters, sealed them, for more than a year. I saw a great deal."

"So that's why the Pei family wants to buy you."

"Not only the Pei family," Qingluo said. "While I was at the Court of the National Granaries, people came asking about me even then."

She glanced toward the front courtyard, then toward the gate, making sure no one was around. Only then did she step two paces closer and lower her voice even further.

"The black lacquer box Mr. Zhou was carrying this evening—I don't know the box. But the seal pressed into the clay on its lid—that, this maid knows."

Her fingers tightened on the lamp.

"It is the personal seal of the Crown Prince's Librarian."

Shen You's brows twitched.

The Crown Prince's Librarian. Li Jiancheng's Eastern Palace had once had a Librarian whose name everyone knew.

"...Wei Zheng?"

Qingluo nodded.
