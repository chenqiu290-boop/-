# Chapter 6: The Chief Constable

The Hour of the Rat. The fifth day.

Five people stepped out of the darkness and stood in the main hall. The house was bigger now. Nobody was crowded at all.

[Deathsworn · Thirteen]

[Skill: Mounted Archery (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Fourteen]

[Skill: Perfumery (Grandmaster)]

The archer was a tall young man. The first thing he asked when he got to his feet was, "Is there a bow in the house?"

"No," Shen You said. "But today, odds are somebody's coming to call."

"Then I'll need one before dawn." He turned to look at Zhou Five.

Zhou Five was already picking through the lumber. Feng One, the smith, broad in the shoulders and thick through the middle, heard that arrowheads were wanted and, without a word, dragged Zheng Two out to the back courtyard to build a forge.

The perfumer was a girl with a sharp little nose. She didn't rush to speak. She walked up to Shen You, sniffed delicately, and took half a step back.

"Master," she said, very tactfully, "how many days has it been since you bathed?"

Shen You counted on his fingers. Five days since he'd crossed over, and not one bath.

"...I'll wash at first light."

Zheng Four, Zheng Five, Zheng Six, Zheng Seven: week two was fully booked. The smith was named Feng One. Week three, now open for business.

Fifteen.

***

Before dawn, Shen You went through last night's pile of stolen goods one more time.

"Qian Biao will come," he said. "He's an official—he won't climb the wall. He'll come through the front gate with a writ from the county and his constables behind him."

"On what grounds?" Zheng Three asked.

"On these." Shen You nudged the heap of silk with his foot. "Someone files an accusation: the monk who just returned to lay life and moved into Kaiming Ward is harboring stolen goods. He brings his men to search, and what do you know, there's loot in the woodshed. Caught red-handed. First he locks me up in the county jail and seals the house, and then he can take his sweet time looking for his box."

Zhou One's brow twitched. "Stolen goods he hid himself, used to frame the Master."

"Not a stupid move," Shen You said. "Too bad he doesn't know those three last night told us everything."

"Then this pile..."

Shen You looked up at the great beam of the main hall.

"Two months they searched, and not once did they look up." He smiled. "This time, let's not have them look up either."

Zhou Five climbed onto the beam while Zhou Four and Zheng Two handed things up from below. Six bolts of silk, two copper basins, a bundle of jewelry, several strings of coins—one by one, everything was stuffed into the gap between the beam ends and the rafters, then covered over tight with old mats. Looking up from below, all you could see was dark timber and cobwebs.

***

When the sun came up, Shen You was soaking in his first hot bath since landing in the Tang.

Zhou Five had finished a bow before dawn. Zheng Two and Feng One had torn down two old door panels and pieced them into a big wooden tub half a man high; Feng One had forged two iron hoops to bind it tight, and it now sat in the empty room next to the kitchen. Zhou Three boiled three big cauldrons of water, grumbling about the cost of firewood the whole time she poured. Zheng Seven mixed bean flour with ground soap pods, angelica root, and a pinch of cloves, and rolled it into little bath beans.

The hot water came up over his shoulders. The bath beans worked into a fine lather that smelled of cloves. Five days of sweat, dirt, and the moldy stink of the ruined temple came off him layer by layer.

Shen You leaned his head back against the rim of the tub, gazed up at the rafters, and let out a long, long sigh.

That was when someone started pounding on the front gate.

"Open up! Wannian County constables, on official business!"

*Bang. Bang. Bang.* The gate panels shook.

Zhou Four appeared in the doorway of the bath room. "They're here. Ten of them. Qian Biao in front, eight constables, and Zhang Xiaoyi from last night."

"Let them wait." Shen You kept his eyes closed. "I'll talk to them when I'm done with my bath."

***

Qian Biao had been pounding on the gate for a quarter of an hour and was just about to have his men break it down.

By the time it opened, his face was as black as the bottom of a pot.

He'd brought eight constables, every one with a *hengdao* at his hip, iron truncheons and coils of rope in hand. Zhang Xiaoyi shrank at the very back, pale-faced, not daring to look up.

Out in the street, a ring of onlookers had already gathered. Warden Zhao had squeezed his way to the front, looking frantic. Sister Liu stood on tiptoe with a winnowing basket in her arms, craning to see inside. Seven or eight of the ward's layabouts whispered among themselves.

On the platform in front of the main hall sat a new chair.

It was elm, a good deal taller than a folding stool, with a backrest and armrests sanded smooth as glass. Zhou Five had knocked it together yesterday evening, modeled on the rope chairs.

Shen You sat in it.

His hair was still wet. He had a clean robe draped loosely over his shoulders and a bowl of hot water in his hands. Sitting there, he was higher than Qian Biao standing in the courtyard.

Qian Biao looked at the chair. The corner of his eye twitched.

"Shen You?"

"That's me. Up bright and early, Chief Qian. What can I do for you?"

Qian Biao pulled a sheet of paper from the front of his robe and snapped it open. "Someone's informed against you for harboring stolen goods. The County Commandant has issued a writ. I'm ordered to search."

"Let me see the writ."

Qian Biao gave a cold laugh and thrust it forward.

Shen You took it and read it word by word.

The writ was genuine, and so was the Wannian County seal. It read: *Per an informant's report: stolen goods are concealed in the woodshed of the residence of Shen You, Kaiming Ward. Chief Constable Qian Biao is directed to go there and search.*

He folded it up and handed it back.

"The writ says the woodshed," he said. "Your informant sure knows a lot—even knows what's in my woodshed. Go right ahead, Chief Qian."

Qian Biao stared at him. This monk was too calm. Calm enough to make the hair on the back of his neck stand up.

But the goods were in the woodshed. His own men had hidden them there with their own hands. They weren't going anywhere.

"Search!"

Four constables charged into the back courtyard.

A moment later came the sound of things being turned over and tossed aside in the woodshed. Then silence.

One of the constables came running back with a strange look on his face. "Chief... there's nothing in the woodshed but wood."

Qian Biao's expression changed.

He whipped around to look at Zhang Xiaoyi. Zhang Xiaoyi's face was even whiter than his. His lips trembled, and he shook his head frantically.

"Impossible," Qian Biao said through his teeth. "The goods were—"

He swallowed the rest.

"The goods were what?" Shen You asked, in no hurry at all.

Qian Biao didn't answer. His eyes swept the courtyard: over the main hall, over the wing rooms, over the overgrown garden out back.

"Not in the woodshed means they've been moved." His voice went cold. "Search! The whole house! Room by room!"

"The writ says the woodshed," Shen You said.

"I say we search, we search!"

*Shing.* The constables drew their *hengdao*.

The crowd outside the gate lurched back a step as one. Warden Zhao stamped his feet in a panic. Sister Liu clapped a hand over her mouth.

Shen You didn't move. He held his bowl of hot water and took a sip.

"Zhou Four."

***

The door of the main hall opened.

The door of the east wing opened.

The door of the west wing opened.

Several people stepped through the moon gate from the back courtyard; several more stepped out from either side of the gatehouse.

One. Two. Three...

Fifteen people, without a sound, lined all four sides of the front courtyard. Some carried planes. Some carried iron tongs. Some carried nothing at all and simply stood there. The last to come out of the main hall was a tall young man holding a newly whittled wooden bow, the wood still raw and pale.

Qian Biao's pupils contracted.

From the very back, Zhang Xiaoyi cried out before he could stop himself. "F-five more! Last night there were only ten!"

"So it's fifteen. So what?" Qian Biao ground out. "I'm an official! You'd dare resist arrest?"

He took a step forward.

*Fwsss—*

Something whistled past his ear.

*Thock.* Qian Biao's *futou* was gone from his head.

He froze. Then, slowly, he turned around.

A wooden arrow had pinned his *futou* to the post of the front gate, the shaft still quivering with a low hum. The arrowhead was freshly forged, the iron a bluish gray, and it had bitten deep into the wood.

The courtyard went dead silent.

Zheng Six stood on the steps of the main hall. He had already nocked a second arrow, its point resting steady on Qian Biao's chest.

Outside the gate, someone sucked in a sharp breath.

"Thirty paces," a voice whispered. "Thirty paces, shot the cap right off a man's head, and didn't touch a single hair..."

Qian Biao stood in the middle of the courtyard with his hair hanging loose, his face going green, then white. The eight constables held their blades up, and not one of them dared take a step.

***

"Easy, Chief Qian."

Shen You set down his bowl, took a sheet of paper from the front of his robe, and shook it open.

"Our three guests left this behind last night," he said. "Zhang Xiaoyi, Li Hei, Wang the Third—thumbprints from all of them. It says: *Acting on the instructions of Chief Constable Qian Biao, for two months entered this residence by night to play at ghosts, frighten off buyers, and search for a certain object. Further, on Qian Biao's instructions, hid stolen goods in the woodshed of this residence. The stolen goods: six bolts of silk, two copper basins, four sets of silver hairpins, one pair of jade bracelets, and a little over three strings of Kaiyuan coins.*"

He raised his eyes to Qian Biao.

"Silver hairpins and jade bracelets—when people report things like that stolen, they always describe exactly what they look like. Chief Qian, shall I take this paper to the County Commandant, then bring that pile out from wherever it's hidden and check it piece by piece against the loss reports at the county office?"

Qian Biao's lips moved, but no sound came out.

His eyes swept the courtyard again. The main hall. The wing rooms. The woodshed. The overgrown garden.

The thing he'd spent two months looking for had still been in this house yesterday. The goods he'd planted had still been in the woodshed last night.

Now he couldn't find any of it.

And the monk sitting in that chair knew everything.

Qian Biao had been a chief constable in Chang'an for a dozen-odd years. He had caught thieves, he had killed men, he had seen situations of every size. He knew when to bite, and when to let go.

He took a deep breath, bent down, picked up the hair ribbon lying on the ground, and tied his hair back any which way.

"...What do you want, young master?"

"Nothing much," Shen You said. "This paper stays with me. So does that pile of goods. From now on, when you're on duty in Kaiming Ward, look out for us a little, Chief Qian, and we'll all get along fine."

Qian Biao stared at him and said nothing.

"One more thing. I'll trouble you to carry a message to that noble patron of yours."

Shen You slipped the black lacquer box out of his sleeve and held it up on his palm, making sure Qian Biao got a good look at the clay seal on the lid.

Qian Biao's eyes locked onto it.

"The box is with me. The seal hasn't been touched, and I don't plan to touch it." Shen You put the box away again. "But if anything should ever happen to me, or to anyone in my household, this box goes, exactly as it is, to Xiande Hall of the Eastern Palace."

Xiande Hall of the Eastern Palace. Where the new emperor had ascended the throne, and where he now attended to affairs of state.

The last of the color drained from Qian Biao's face.

***

Qian Biao left with his men.

He pulled the arrow out of the gatepost with his own hands and took it with him, along with the *futou* it had pierced. He didn't leave a single word behind.

The moment they were gone, the crowd outside the gate burst into a buzz.

"Did you see that? One arrow! Just one!"

"That was Qian Biao! You know what kind of man he is? He's broken three legs catching thieves! And today he didn't dare so much as fart!"

"Those dozen-odd people in Young Master Shen's house—did you get a look? Every last one of them like they were made of iron..."

"I always said that house was a terror," Sister Liu told the people around her, winnowing basket in her arms, glowing with the telling. "Takes a terror to tame one! He's the man to keep it down!"

Warden Zhao squeezed in through the gate and made Shen You a long, deep bow with his hands clasped. He was halfway through it before he remembered what he meant to say, and it came out in a stammer. "Young master... young master, you've had a fright. From now on, anything you need in the ward, you have only to say the word."

Shen You smiled and waved it off. "You're too kind, Warden Zhao."

He leaned back in his chair, hair still wet, and basked in the sun, eyes narrowed in contentment.

Zhou Four came over and stood beside him. "Qian Biao won't leave it there," he said quietly.

"I know."

"The box..."

"That box is my talisman," Shen You said. "The noble patron behind him is afraid of one thing above all, and that's the box going into the Eastern Palace. As long as it stays in my hands, unopened and undelivered, he won't dare come after me in the open."

"And not in the open?"

Shen You didn't answer. He just looked at the fresh arrow hole in the gatepost and smiled.

***

That afternoon, in the small side courtyard of the Dou house.

The man who'd been doing the tailing stood under the pear tree and reported everything, start to finish.

Dou the Eleventh was feeding his falcon. Somewhere in the listening, he forgot to hold out the strip of meat in his hand. The falcon cocked its head and waited a good long while, then gave an impatient cry.

"One arrow, and he pinned Qian Biao's *futou* to the gatepost?"

"Yes. Thirty paces."

"And Qian Biao just left?"

"Left. White as a sheet."

Dou the Eleventh slowly held out the meat to the falcon.

"The day before yesterday," he said, "it was a ruined temple and six people."

"Yesterday he bought the haunted house in the northeast corner. Ten people."

"Today, fifteen."

He turned to look at Fan Da. Every trace of his usual idleness had gone out of his face.

"Fan Da. Tell me—how many people will he have in that house tomorrow?"

Fan Da thought about it and couldn't come up with an answer.

***

That night, Shen You slept like the dead.

A hot bath, soft bedding, a whole day of good sunshine—he was asleep the moment his head touched the pillow.

In the second watch, a tile on the roof of the main hall gave a faint click.

Faint—as faint as a cat's paw passing over it.

But Zhou Four, keeping watch beneath the eaves, opened his eyes.

Without a sound, he edged outward inch by inch, pressed close against a veranda pillar. A dark figure crouched on the ridge of the roof, reaching to lift a second tile.

Zhou Four climbed the pillar, swung up over the eaves, crept bent-backed to just below the ridge, and grabbed for the man's ankle.

The figure reacted uncannily fast. He jerked his foot back, twisted around, and slashed. The gleam of the blade skimmed past Zhou Four's wrist. Zhou Four turned his body aside and drove a fist into the man's ribs. The figure grunted, rode the force of the blow down the far side of the ridge, rolled as he hit the ground, and in a few bounds was up on the back wall.

By the time Zhou Four reached the top of the wall, the man had vanished into the dark.

Lamps came on in the front courtyard. Shen You came out with his clothes thrown over his shoulders, Zhou One and Zheng Six behind him.

"Got away." Zhou Four jumped down from the wall and looked at his wrist. His sleeve had been slit open, but the blade hadn't touched skin. "Not one of Qian Biao's layabouts. Fights like a soldier. Knows his blade."

"Soldier from where?"

Zhou Four was silent for a moment.

"The box is the Eastern Palace's. Most likely he's one of the men scattered from the Eastern Palace Guard."

On the post of the front gate, there was something new.

A short knife, driven in beside the arrow hole from that morning, pinning a slip of paper beneath it.

Zhou One took down the slip, held it close to the lamp, and read it aloud:

"Within ten days, hand over the box."

The front courtyard went quiet. All fifteen of them were looking at Shen You.

Shen You took the slip and glanced at it, then looked up at the short knife, and suddenly laughed out loud.

"Ten days?"

He crumpled the paper into a ball and tossed it away.

"In ten days, there'll be a hundred and twenty people in my house."
