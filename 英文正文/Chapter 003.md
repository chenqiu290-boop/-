# Chapter 3: Shouting for Lu

The Hour of the Rat. The third day.

Three people stepped out of the darkness and dropped to one knee.

"Greetings, Master."

This time Shen You didn't need the lighter. Zhou One had already banked a small fire in the middle of the ruined temple, and its light picked out three faces.

[Deathsworn · Four]

[Skill: Hand-to-Hand Combat (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Five]

[Skill: Carpentry (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Six]

[Skill: Medicine (Grandmaster)]

The fighter was a man of thirty or so, not especially tall but broad across the shoulders, with a layer of old calluses on the backs of his hands. When he got to his feet, he didn't drift toward the fire. He walked a circuit of the temple first, looked over the front door, then the half-collapsed gap in the back wall.

"None of the Master's people here can fight," he said when he came back. "One man can't hold the front door. The back wall needs blocking up."

With that, he went to the doorway and said to Zhou One, "Your eyes are red from staying up. I'll take over."

Zhou One looked at him, didn't argue, and got up to give him the spot.

The carpenter was a gray-bearded old man in his forties. The first thing he did when he came in was look up at the roof beam. He studied it for a long while, then reached out and rapped on a pillar, listened to the sound, and shook his head.

"The worms have eaten this beam half hollow. It won't make it through the winter," he told Shen You. "Not worth fixing. If the Master means to stay long, better find somewhere else."

Shen You nodded. "That's the plan."

The physician was a woman just past twenty, slender, with pale, quiet features. She was in no hurry to speak. She looked over every person in the temple one by one, and her gaze finally came to rest on Zhou One's face.

"This gentleman has a bluish cast under his eyes. He's gone more than one night without sleep."

Then she turned to Shen You.

"The Master has come from far away, and his body hasn't taken to the local water yet."

"I know." Shen You pointed at the clay pot beside the fire. "Since yesterday, Sanniang's been boiling every drop I drink."

Who knew what was living in well water from fourteen hundred years ago? He had that much common sense, at least.

Beside him, Zhou Three muttered, "Firewood money's up by half."

Zhou Six glanced at Shen You, looking a little surprised, and nodded.

"Then there's one thing still missing. While the Master was talking just now, he pressed a hand to his stomach twice," she said. "The water's boiled and the food's changed, and the stomach can't catch up all at once. When it's light, I'll go to the medicine sellers for some agastache and dried tangerine peel. Boil it in water and drink it for two days."

Shen You looked down at his own hand.

He hadn't even noticed.

"I'll trouble you, then."

"Zhou Four, Zhou Five, Zhou Six." He pointed to each of them in turn.

The three of them answered one after another.

What came after Zhou Seven, once the week ran out, was tomorrow's headache.

***

Before dawn, Shen You had handed out the work.

Zhou One would stay at the temple to carve the seals and make the travel passes, all of it done before the day was out. Zhou Three would take Zhou Five to the West Market to find a blacksmith for the wok and have a look at timber while they were at it. Zhou Six would go to the medicine sellers and bring back some common herbs.

Zhou Two would go to the gambling den. Zhou Four would go with him.

"I'm coming too," Shen You said.

Zhou Two raised his drooping eyelids. "If the Master comes, I'll have to split my attention."

Zhou Four spoke up as well. "Fewer people, easier to get out."

"I won't bet, I won't talk. I'll just sit off to the side and have a drink."

"Why does the Master have to go?"

"Because it's fun," Shen You said, without a shred of shame.

Zhou Two didn't make a sound. He just looked at him.

Shen You laughed and tried a more serious answer. "Think about it. You go in alone and win money, and you're a strange face come to break the house. You bring Zhou Four, and it's a gambler and his muscle. Even worse."

"With me there, it's different. A young gentleman out for a bit of fun with two servants, and one of his men has a lucky hand and wins some money. The den's seen plenty of those. Nobody will think twice."

Zhou Two thought it over and gave a single nod.

"Then the Master sits well back."

***

The moment the ward gate opened, the three of them left the ruined temple.

Kaiming Ward in the early morning wasn't the same place it had been the evening before. A thin mist clung to the millet fields, and people were already out harvesting. A whole family stood in a line, the man in front cutting, the woman behind him binding, while a bare-bottomed little boy hauled a bundle of millet stalks taller than himself out to the ridge between the fields. He tripped, fell flat, got up, and kept hauling.

At a well by the road, a woman was cranking up water on the windlass. When she saw three strange faces, she stopped and looked Shen You up and down a couple of times.

"New here, young master?"

"Sort of. I'm in that ruined temple to the east."

"That temple leaks," the woman said flatly. Then she jerked her chin at the well. "The water in this one's bitter. Rice comes out tasting rough. Go north past two houses, and the one at the foot of the wall's sweeter. Its rope's too short, though. Bring your own."

That said, she hooked her buckets onto her shoulder pole and carried them off. A few steps on, she turned and added, "That graveyard behind the temple. Stay out of it at night. There's foxes."

Shen You stood there for a good while before he started laughing.

At the ward gate, the ward warden was quarreling with a man selling sesame flatbreads from a carrying pole.

"Eight coins yesterday, and ten today?"

"Wheat's gone up!" The flatbread seller's voice was even louder than his. "Once word got out the north's going to war, the price of wheat in the West Market changes every day. My flatbread's made from flour ground out of wheat, not blown together out of wind!"

"War? Is it coming to Chang'an?"

"War or no war, the grain dealers don't care. Raise the price first and worry later!"

Grumbling and cursing, the warden counted out ten coins, took a flatbread, and bit into it. Then he found it too hot and bared his teeth, blowing on it.

In the patrol post beside the gate, a patrolman sat on the threshold, also gnawing on a flatbread. He lifted his eyes and ran them over the *futou* on Shen You's head, then over the two men behind him, and turned to say something to his partner inside.

Shen You passed just in time to hear the second half: "...the general amnesty let out a whole crowd of them. There's new faces all over the ward these days. How're we supposed to question them all?"

Someone inside grunted lazily in reply.

The patrolman lowered his head and went back to his flatbread.

Shen You gave him a nod and walked out the ward gate.

Then he stopped at the edge of Vermilion Bird Avenue.

The day before yesterday, he'd seen it from a distance, from the top of that earthen slope, and had only thought it wide. Standing right in front of it, he realized "wide" wasn't the word.

He'd walked Chang'an Avenue in Beijing. This street was wider than Chang'an Avenue.

The roadbed was rammed yellow earth, cut with deep ruts from cartwheels. On both sides ran drainage ditches deeper than a man was tall, and along their edges stood rows of pagoda trees, their branches hung thick with seedpods. People were already out in the early morning: farmers driving oxcarts into the city, peddlers with loads on their shoulder poles, officials on horseback with servants in tow. Wherever hooves passed, a cloud of yellow dust went up.

Zhou Four stepped half a pace forward and put himself on the upwind side, between Shen You and the dust.

Shen You narrowed his eyes, took a look around, and noticed something strange.

On the whole avenue, there wasn't a single shop.

There were only ward walls on either side, and in the walls only ward gates. No signboards, no storefronts, no hawkers calling out. Walking down the street was like walking between two endless rows of walls.

He mulled it over for a moment and smiled.

Put plainly, the whole of Chang'an was a hundred-odd gated communities plus two shopping malls.

"What's the Master smiling at?" Zhou Two asked.

"At how neatly they laid this city out."

Two hundred years from now, a man named Bai Juyi would stand on high ground in the south of the city, look down, and write two lines of verse: *A hundred thousand homes like a board of Go; twelve avenues like rows in a vegetable patch.* Back when Shen You first read them, they hadn't meant much. Now, standing on this street, he knew there wasn't a word of exaggeration in them.

***

They headed north along Vermilion Bird Avenue. They had only just passed Lanling Ward when a bell rang out from behind the ward wall on their right.

*Bong—*

The sound was deep, coming one stroke at a time, as if it were welling up from under the ground.

"Daxingshan Temple," Zhou Two said.

Shen You looked to his left. Across an avenue more than a hundred paces wide stood a yellow earthen ward wall, with a few corners of upswept eaves showing above it.

"That's the Xuandu Daoist temple," he said.

Zhou Two looked at him.

"One Buddhist temple and one Daoist temple, facing each other east and west across Vermilion Bird Avenue." Shen You clasped his hands behind his back and strolled along at an easy pace. "The men who built this city back under the Sui said there were six high ridges in it, like the six lines of the Qian hexagram. The emperor lived on Nine-Two, the officials on Nine-Three. Nine-Five was the seat of honor, no place for ordinary people to live, so they built a Buddhist temple and a Daoist temple here to hold it down."

Zhou Two said, "The Master knows a lot."

"Watched videos."

"...Videos?"

"A kind of book."

Four more wards to the north, and the south wall of the Imperial City lay across their path.

It was much taller than the outer city wall, with a row of crenellations along the top like a row of teeth. In the middle stood Vermilion Bird Gate, its gate tower soaring high, its double eaves sweeping up. The people going in and out of its passage were all officials and clerks on their way to their bureaus, in crimson robes and green robes, some on horseback and some in carriages, with servants trotting ahead to clear the way.

Beyond the Imperial City, he could see rooftops even farther north. That was the Palace City.

The Retired Emperor still lived in Taiji Palace, right in the center. The new emperor had taken the throne in the Eastern Palace, and was most likely handling affairs there right now. Shen You looked at that stretch of rooftops to the east and thought: day three on the job. The man was probably run off his feet.

Shen You, on the other hand, was here for the show.

***

At Vermilion Bird Gate, Zhou Two led them west along the avenue outside the Imperial City's south wall for a stretch, then into a ward across the street east of the West Market.

This was a different world from the south of the city.

The ward was packed to bursting. The winding lanes were narrow and deep, the courtyard walls on either side close enough to brush your shoulders. There were people everywhere: men driving donkeys laden with goods, laborers squatting at the foot of the walls waiting for work, people with children in their arms having a shouting match. The air was a mix of spices, livestock, and slop.

After a great many twists and turns, Zhou Two stopped in front of an unremarkable black-lacquered wooden door.

A sturdy, hard-muscled man squatted by the door, chewing on a handful of jujubes. When he saw the three of them, his eyelids lifted.

Zhou Two fished out a string of coins, counted off ten, and set them by the man's feet.

The man swept the coins into his sleeve and recognized Zhou Two. "You're the one who came by yesterday, walked around, and never put down a bet? Brought company today." He shifted his backside to one side and spat out a jujube pit. "Don't make trouble in there. Fan Da's in today."

"Who's Fan Da?" Shen You asked under his breath.

"Runs the place," Zhou Two said. "Didn't see him yesterday."

***

They crossed a small courtyard and pushed aside a heavy door curtain, and the heat and the din hit them full in the face.

Every window in the main hall had been blocked off with straw blinds. In broad daylight, a dozen-odd oil lamps were burning, and their smoke had blackened the beams. Thirty or forty people were crammed inside, and the smells of sweat, wine, and lamp oil churned together, so acrid that Shen You's eyes narrowed.

Several big tables stood in the middle, ringed so tightly not a drop of water could have gotten through.

"*Lu!* Lu-u-u!"

A man with a face full of stubble had a handful of wooden pieces clenched in his fist and raised high over his head, the veins jumping on his forehead. The ring of people around him roared along with him, roaring until the rafters shook.

The pieces scattered into the wooden tray. Five of them went tumbling around it.

They came to rest. Three black, two white.

"Aww—" A chorus of sighs.

The stubbled man slapped his thigh, swore, and shoved the string of coins in front of him across the table.

"Wang Eldest!" someone jeered. "Your mother told you to sell the donkey and buy wheat seed. How much of the donkey money's left?"

"Get lost!" Wang the Eldest's eyes were bloodshot. He fished out another small string of coins and slapped it on the table. "One more round! Once the *lu* comes up, I'm buying two donkeys!"

Now Shen You understood. This was *chupu*. Five wooden pieces, black on one side and white on the other. All five black was called *lu*, the best throw there was, so everyone shouted for *lu*. An old idiom came to Shen You's mind: *hulu hezhi*, shouting for *lu* and hollering for *zhi*. That was exactly the scene in front of him.

At several smaller tables along the sides, people were playing *shuanglu*, and another group was gathered around a coarse earthenware bowl, throwing dice. Strings of Kaiyuan Tongbao coins were heaped on the tables, though some people had bet bolts of silk instead, folded into neat squares and set at the corner of the table.

There were gamblers of every kind: a peddler in a short tunic of coarse cloth; a militiaman in a half-worn army robe, likely home on leave; household servants in bright, fine clothes; and a curly-haired Hu merchant sitting in a corner. He didn't smile when he won and didn't scowl when he lost. Before every throw, he blew into his palm and touched a small bronze tag that hung from his neck.

Shen You found a folding stool by the wall, sat down, and ordered a bowl of cloudy wine.

When the wine came, it was a murky yellow, sweet with a sour edge. He took a sip and set it down.

Zhou Four took up a position behind him, like a post driven into the ground.

Zhou Two had already squeezed his way in at the *chupu* table.

***

The way Zhou Two gambled didn't look like a grandmaster's at all.

The first few rounds, he lost. Not much, thirty coins, fifty coins, and when he lost, he scratched his head and looked thoroughly put out.

Then he started winning. Not much either. Win three, lose one. The rounds he lost were always small bets, and the rounds he won, each bet was bigger than the last.

He never rushed to throw. He always waited until the others had thrown before he bet. Nor did he bet against everyone. Sometimes a whole round would go by the circle of players and he'd bet against only two or three of them.

It took Shen You a while to see the trick. Zhou Two only picked the ones who'd gotten desperate. The more they lost, the more desperate they got; the more desperate they got, the bigger they bet; and the bigger they bet, the more easily they lost to him.

And yet Wang the Eldest, the most desperate man in the room, Zhou Two hadn't bet against even once.

The rule here was that the house didn't gamble against its customers. It only took a cut. What Zhou Two was winning was the money of every gambler in the room.

More than a double-hour went by, and the strings of coins in front of Zhou Two quietly stacked up higher.

Shen You held the bowl of cloudy wine he'd barely touched, but his eyes weren't on Zhou Two.

He was watching someone.

The man was thirty or so, in a nondescript gray robe, with an old scar across his left eyebrow. He'd been leaning against a pillar in the corner of the room the whole time. Everyone else in the room had their eyes fixed on the wooden trays; he alone watched the people. Who bet how much, who was losing so badly his eyes had gone red, whose hand had slipped back into his sleeve for a moment, he took it all in.

The clearest head in this room was his.

His gaze lingered on Zhou Two longer and longer.

Then he turned his head and met Shen You's eyes.

Across half a room of lamp smoke, the two of them looked at each other.

The man smiled, straightened up, and came over.

***

"Not going to play a few rounds, young master?" He sat down on the folding stool next to Shen You's.

"Don't know how."

"If you don't know how to play, what brings you to a gambling den?"

"The show."

The scar-browed man tipped his chin toward the *chupu* table. "That young fellow over there. One of your household?"

"He is. His hands were itching, so I brought him out to loosen up."

"He's loosened up, all right." The scar-browed man smiled. "Wins three, loses one. Small bets when he loses, big bets when he wins. As of now, not a coin more or less, he's won exactly three times his stake."

Shen You turned his head and looked at him. "You keep a sharp count, Manager."

"In this line of work, if you can't count, you starve." He paused. "The name's Fan. I've seen plenty of lucky hands. A lucky hand that plays by the rules this well, that I've never seen."

"Is playing by the rules a bad thing?"

"It's a good thing," Manager Fan said. "The ones who don't play by the rules win one throw and want to win the whole room, then lose everything, leave, and never come back. The ones who play by the rules are the ones who keep coming."

"So do you want him to win more, Manager, or lose more?"

"I want him to come more."

"That depends on whether he's having fun."

Manager Fan laughed out loud, not offended in the least.

"Which ward is your household in, young master?"

"South of the city."

"Which ward in the south?"

"The one that grows millet."

Manager Fan was taken aback for a moment, then laughed again, shaking his head, and didn't press any further.

Just then, over at the *chupu* table, Zhou Two swept up the strings of coins in front of him and stood.

"I'm done."

Somebody didn't like that. "Win and walk?"

Zhou Two yawned. "Getting on toward noon. Family's waiting on lunch."

He slung the strings of coins over his shoulder, picked up two bolts of silk he'd won from the corner of the table, and ambled back to Shen You's side.

Shen You knew exactly what that meant. The moment Manager Fan sat down next to him, it was time for Zhou Two to stop. Shen You didn't need to give him a look. Zhou Two could see it for himself.

"Let's go."

Manager Fan rose and cupped his hands. "Take care on your way, young master."

***

Out the black-lacquered door, the three of them turned into the winding lane they'd come in by.

"That Wang the Eldest," Shen You said suddenly. "You didn't touch him once."

Zhou Two's eyelids drooped. "That was donkey money. Win it, and it wouldn't spend right."

The lane was narrow and long, the courtyard walls on either side higher than their heads, and halfway along it bent around a corner.

When they reached the bend, Zhou Four stopped short.

Three men stood at the mouth of the lane ahead, arms folded, blocking it completely.

Shen You looked back. Behind them, at some point, three more had appeared.

Six men. None of them carrying anything. They just stood there.

Zhou Four moved one step to the side so that Shen You had his back to the courtyard wall, and planted himself in the middle of the lane. Zhou Two shifted the strings of coins from his shoulder to his left hand and slid his right hand into his sleeve.

Footsteps came from behind them, unhurried.

Manager Fan stepped out from behind the men blocking the way, still wearing that same smile.

"A moment, young master."

"Is there something else, Manager?"

"Rules." Manager Fan cupped his hands. "A new face comes in for the first time and walks off with the whole room's money. You'll have to come with me to see my master and make it clear whose household you belong to."

He sighed, as if this were a matter that couldn't be helped.

"Otherwise, by tomorrow every gambling den in Chang'an will be saying somebody broke my house, and I didn't so much as fart about it. After that, this is a living I couldn't make anymore."

Shen You didn't answer. First he looked at Zhou Four.

Zhou Four's voice was pitched so low that only Shen You could hear it. "Six. Two have trained. Four just swing their fists. If the Master wants to go, we can go."

Shen You nodded and turned back to face Manager Fan.

"And if I don't come?"

"Then I'll have to be rude."

The words were barely out of his mouth when a dark-faced man in the front group ran out of patience. He'd probably had enough of Manager Fan's talk. He took one stride forward and reached straight for the strings of coins in Zhou Two's hand.

His hand never touched the money.

Zhou Four moved. No one saw how. There was only a dull *thud*, and the dark-faced man slammed sideways into the courtyard wall so hard that the plaster came pattering down. He slid down the wall, clutching his arm, and for a long while couldn't get his breath.

Another man bellowed and charged. Zhou Four turned sideways and let him go by, hooked a foot, and drove an elbow down. The man went face-first into the yellow dirt and came up with a mouthful of mud.

The whole thing took no more than two or three breaths.

The remaining four froze where they stood. One of them took half a step forward, then quietly drew it back.

Zhou Four stepped back into place in front of Shen You, his breathing not even ruffled, as if nothing had happened.

Manager Fan looked at the two on the ground. His eyebrow twitched. He raised a hand and pressed it downward. "Nobody move."

Only then did Shen You speak, as casually as before. "Earlier, Manager, you said you wanted him to come more." He tilted his head toward Zhou Two. "Start a fight today and he'll never come again. Have you done that math?"

"I have." Manager Fan glanced at the two men at the foot of the wall and gave a wry smile. "That's why I only brought six men, and no blades."

He paused, then added, "Looks like twelve wouldn't have been enough either."

The lane went quiet for a while.

Shen You looked at the scar-browed man and suddenly found this very interesting.

The rules had to be kept. Face had to be saved. The guest had to be held. And no blood could be spilled. Six men, not too many and not too few, exactly enough to get the words out without pushing things past the point of no return.

This was a man who knew how things worked.

And the "master" behind a man like that was probably no fool either.

Shen You would be in Chang'an for a long time. Sooner or later he'd need to know who was behind these back-alley dens. If someone was inviting him to their door today, that saved him the trouble of asking around.

As for danger—

Six unarmed men, a manager who didn't want blood spilled, broad daylight, right in the middle of Chang'an.

All he had to do today was stay steady.

"Fine." Shen You smiled. "When someone invites you over for tea, turning them down just looks petty."

Zhou Two and Zhou Four exchanged a look. Neither said a word. Zhou Four moved half a step closer to Shen You.

Manager Fan smiled too, and stepped aside to clear the way. "Young master, please."

***

Manager Fan led them out of the ward and north.

After they had passed through two wards, the surroundings gradually grew quiet. There were fewer people in the streets, and what showed above the ward walls now were tall roof ridges and neat rows of eave tiles. Now and then a carriage rolled past, its curtains drawn low, decently dressed servants walking ahead of it.

They turned another corner, and a great mansion came into view.

The main entrance was a *wutou* gate, its two posts lacquered a glossy, gleaming black. Before the gate, to the left and right, stood two rows of wooden racks set with long halberds, their points aimed at the sky, flashing cold in the sun.

Shen You's step slowed by half a beat.

Halberds arrayed before the gate. That was a display only officials of the third rank and above were entitled to.

Manager Fan didn't go to the main gate. He led the three of them around half the length of the courtyard wall and into a quiet, winding lane, stopped before a small side door, and raised his hand to knock three times.

The door opened a crack.

Shen You looked back for one more glance at that row of long halberds.

The corner of his mouth curled up, little by little.

Interesting.
