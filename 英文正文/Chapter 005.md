# Chapter 5: The Haunted House

The Hour of the Rat. The fourth day.

Four people stepped out of the darkness, and all at once the ruined temple was crowded. Even the spot by the door where Zhou Four kept watch lost half its space.

[Deathsworn · Seven]

[Skill: Vocal Mimicry (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Eight]

[Skill: Interpreting (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Nine]

[Skill: Masonry (Grandmaster)]

[Deathsworn · Ten]

[Skill: Accounting (Grandmaster)]

The mimic was a skinny little fellow of seventeen or eighteen, his eyes darting every which way. When he got to his feet, he took one look at Shen You, cleared his throat, and opened his mouth:

"Get up and talk. What can you do?"

The voice, the tone, that lazy drawl—it was Shen You to the life.

Zhou Three burst out laughing. Shen You was tickled too. "All right. No need to tell me what you can do."

The interpreter was pale and clean-cut and spoke at an unhurried pace; he knew the Turkic tongue, Sogdian, and Persian. The mason crouched down the moment he came through the door and ran his hand along the base of the wall. It came away covered in damp earth, and he kept shaking his head. The accountant was a round-faced woman. The first thing she said when she stood up was, "Master, how much money does the household have right now?"

The mimic became Zhou Seven. That made a full week, so the other three started over under a new surname: Zheng One, Zheng Two, Zheng Three.

To Tang ears, a Zhou family with children numbered all the way to seven, and now a Zheng family on top of it, was as ordinary as anything. Only Shen You knew what this really was: week two.

***

At daybreak, Zheng Three crouched on the ground and laid out her counting rods. "The gold ingot comes to twelve strings. Copper coins, one string and three hundred coins. Two bolts of silk. All told, under fifteen strings. Ten people—food alone runs two hundred coins a day."

"Zhou Two goes back out to win today," Shen You said. "I'm going to buy a house."

"Fifteen strings won't buy any kind of decent house."

"Then I'll buy a cheap one."

***

The cheap house came from Sister Liu.

"There's one in the northeast corner of the ward. Big courtyard, its own well, and nobody'll take it even at twenty strings." Sister Liu was crouched in her doorway rubbing millet off the ears, and she dropped her voice. "It's haunted."

It had belonged to a minor official of the Eastern Palace. In the sixth month there had been the trouble at Xuanwu Gate, and on the night the news reached him, the man hanged himself from the beam in his main hall. His family, terrified, fled back to Hedong that same night and left the place with a broker to sell. Two months on the market, and no one dared take it.

"Folks who pass that gate at night all say they hear somebody sighing in there." Sister Liu shot a glance off to the northeast. "And some have seen a light behind the paper windows, flickering back and forth."

"A light?"

"Sure as I'm sitting here. Ghosts lighting lamps—now isn't that strange?" She shivered, then something else came back to her. "Oh, and a while ago Qian Biao, the chief constable from the county, came round asking about that house too. Brought a few men and walked circles round the gate a good many times. Even he was scared. Didn't dare go in."

Shen You nodded, took a small string of coins from his sleeve, and set it down beside her basket of millet.

Ghosts that sighed. Ghosts that lit lamps. A chief constable who walked circles round the gate.

This house was interesting.

***

The moment Warden Zhao heard someone wanted to buy the house in the northeast corner, he didn't waste a word—he hurried off to neighboring Baoning Ward and fetched the broker himself. That empty house drew wild dogs and thieves at night, and as ward warden he'd been losing sleep over it.

The broker's name was He. He had a round face and a sweat cloth tucked into his belt, and the whole way over he mopped his brow and talked about house prices in Chang'an: lately the houses in the northern wards had gotten frighteningly expensive, what with the Zhangsun family's relations and the old hands from the Prince of Qin's household all scrambling for them. Nobody wanted the ones south of the city—least of all that one.

At the gate, He the broker unlocked it, then stood outside the threshold and didn't budge.

"Go on in and have a look yourself, young master. I'll wait right here."

Warden Zhao stopped as well, hands clasped behind his back, studying the sky.

Shen You went in with Zhou Four, Zhou Five, and Zheng Two.

The house faced south, its back to the north, two courtyards deep, front and back, on a plot of nearly three mu.

Inside the entrance was a three-bay gatehouse. Through it lay the front courtyard, perfectly square, with three wing rooms on the east side and three on the west. Due north stood the main hall, three bays wide and five purlins deep, its platform raised two chi above the courtyard, a covered veranda running along its front. By regulation, an official of the seventh rank or below could build his main hall no bigger than three bays and five purlins—He the broker had made a point of mentioning it at the gate. Behind the main hall was the rear courtyard, even bigger than the front: a three-bay sleeping hall along the north side, the kitchen and woodshed on the east, and on the west a stable with room to tie up four or five horses. All the rest was overgrown garden, wild mugwort grown waist-high, and in the middle of it a well covered with a slab of bluestone.

Front and back, large and small, there were twenty-odd rooms in all. Thirty or fifty people could live here with room to spare.

Shen You pushed open the door of the main hall.

The hall was empty and coated in a layer of dust. On the beam right in the middle, a rope mark had bitten deep into the wood.

Outside the gate, He the broker held his breath.

But Shen You wasn't looking at the beam. His head was down. He was looking at the floor.

The dust on the floor was wrong.

With no one living in the hall for two months, the dust should have lain in one flat, even layer. But on this layer there were footprints, trail after trail of them, running from the door to the corner and then looping back from the corner again. They were big prints, with patterned soles. Boots. In the corner, three or four floor bricks had been pried up and then jammed carelessly back in place. The dirt in the cracks was still fresh.

He went to the kitchen, crouched down, and reached into the stove to feel the ash.

Underneath, it still held a little warmth.

"Zhou Four," he said quietly.

"I see it." Zhou Four kept his voice down too. "Three at least. They come often. Last time was last night."

"What kind of people?"

"People who wear boots."

Shen You stood up and brushed the ash from his hands, and the corners of his mouth curled up.

The sighing was people. The lamplight was people. The ones prying up floor bricks were people too.

Someone was using a ghost to scare off buyers, while they themselves came to this house every night, searching for something.

Two months of searching, and they still hadn't found it.

He walked back out to the gate. He the broker asked, ever so carefully, "You didn't... hear anything, did you, young master?"

"I did."

He the broker's knees went weak.

"I heard a cat."

Warden Zhao let out a snort of laughter. He the broker mopped his sweat, gave a couple of dry laughs, and slapped his thigh. "Oh, how could I forget—young master's been a monk! A man who's chanted the sutras, he can keep them down, he can keep them down!"

***

He the broker asked thirty strings. Zheng Three saw straight through him in a single sentence: the house had been listed for two months without a sale, the owners were back in Hedong waiting on the money, their floor price was twenty strings, and the extra ten were his own.

Sweat broke out all over He the broker's face.

"Twenty strings, every last coin of it to the owners," Shen You said. "I'll pay your broker's fee separately."

He the broker stood there dumbstruck for a long moment, then gave him a long, deep bow with his hands clasped before him. "You're a decent man, young master."

The deed was drawn up at Warden Zhao's house. They were halfway through writing it when Zhou Two turned up with a porter in tow, a shoulder pole across the man's back—coins hanging from one end, silk from the other. Zhou Two had him set it all down at Zheng Three's feet, said, "Two houses. Eleven strings," and turned around and left again. Now there was enough.

Writing up the deed took a little under half a double-hour, and the whole time everyone sat kneeling on mats. By the end, Shen You had lost all feeling in both legs. When he stood up, his vision went black, and he swayed a couple of times, clutching the door frame.

Understanding dawned on He the broker. "Of course—a monk! Used to sitting cross-legged. Can't take kneeling!"

Shen You nodded with a perfectly straight face. "Exactly so."

Once they were out the door, he said to Zhou Five, "When we get back, make me a chair. With a backrest. The kind you sit in with your legs hanging down."

Zhou Five thought it over. "Like the rope chairs the monks sit in at the temples?"

Shen You blinked, then laughed. These days only monks sat in rope chairs with backrests. A "returned monk" like him sitting in a chair—nobody could find a thing wrong with that.

***

Registering the transfer meant a trip to the Wannian County office.

The clerk of the household registry had dark rings under his eyes, and his desk was piled high with documents. Muttering, "Another one returned to lay life—over a hundred registered this month," he leafed through Shen You's identity papers and asked a few questions: where he'd taken his vows, his master's Dharma name, when he'd come to the capital. Zhou One had run through all of it with Shen You three times the night before, and he answered neither too fast nor too slow.

The clerk found nothing wrong and drew out a sheet of register paper.

"Since you've bought a house in Kaiming Ward, we'll put you on the Wannian County register. Kaiming Ward. Head of household Shen You, age twenty-nine, native of Chengdu County, Yizhou. Monk returned to lay life. Newly registered."

*Thunk.* Down came the county seal.

The papers Zhou One had made were fake. But this sheet of register paper had been written out by a Wannian County clerk's own hand and stamped with the seal by his own hand.

The fake had become real.

"The northeast corner of Kaiming Ward?"

The voice came from off to one side.

Shen You turned his head.

A man was standing under the covered walkway, around forty, very tall, dressed all in black, a straight *hengdao* sword at his hip. His face was long, and an old scar pulled the left corner of his mouth downward, so that he always looked as if he were sneering.

He had probably come to the county office to report in, and just happened to catch what the clerk had said.

"That haunted house," the man said, his eyes fixed on Shen You. "You bought it?"

"I did."

The man said nothing more. He simply looked at him—from his *futou* down to his boots, then from his boots back up to his face, as if he meant to carve him into his memory.

Then he turned and walked away.

Behind Shen You, the clerk said under his breath, "That was Qian Biao, the county's chief constable. From now on, young master... bar your gate at night."

***

At dusk, all ten of them moved into the house in the northeast corner.

They didn't have much, and it took a single trip. Zhou Three went to set the kitchen in order, Zheng Two climbed onto the roof to patch the tiles, and Zhou Four walked the front and rear courtyards twice over, finally stopping at the door of the woodshed.

Behind the woodpile in the shed was a heap of things under straw mats. He pulled the mats back: six bolts of silk, two copper basins, and a cloth bundle. In the bundle were several sets of silver hairpins, a pair of jade bracelets, and a few strings of Kaiyuan Tongbao coins threaded on red cord—three or four strings' worth at the very least.

"Stolen goods," Zhou Four said. "The ones who come at night use this place as a stash."

Shen You looked at the pile. Before he could say anything, Zhou Five's voice came from the main hall.

"Master!"

Zhou Five was straddling the beam in the main hall, holding up a lacquer box about the size of his palm.

"There's a hidden compartment at the end of the beam."

The box was black lacquer, its lid sealed with clay, and pressed into the clay was a small personal seal. Zhou One leaned in to look. "The style the Eastern Palace people commonly used."

The front courtyard went quiet all at once.

Shen You took the box and hefted it. It was very light.

He looked up at the beam, then down at the pried-up bricks in the corner, and all of a sudden he laughed out loud.

"They spent two months prying up floor bricks."

The thing had been hidden up in the beam, and those men had been searching under the floor the whole time. Not one of them had thought to look up.

A minor Eastern Palace official, on the night the news of Xuanwu Gate reached him, before he hanged himself, had hidden a box inside the very beam he hanged himself from. For two months, someone had been using talk of ghosts as cover to ransack this house night after night. The county's chief constable had personally walked circles around the gate.

Whatever was in this box, it had to be deadly.

"Master, do we open it?" Zhou One asked.

"No." Shen You handed the box to him. "Put it away somewhere safe. The Eastern Palace has only just fallen, and the new emperor has barely taken his seat. Anything pulled out of a dead man's beam right now is too hot to hold."

He glanced at the sky.

"Tonight," he said, "we're having guests."

***

The second watch.

Kaiming Ward lay sunk in pitch darkness. Even the dogs had stopped barking.

In the house in the northeast corner, there wasn't a single light.

At the foot of the rear wall, three dark shapes came over the top and landed softly. The leader handed out the jobs in a low voice. "I'll take the main hall. Wang the Third, front courtyard. Zhang Xiaoyi, you go to the well." One crept toward the main hall, one headed for the front courtyard, and the last crouched by the well in the rear courtyard, pulled a piece of white cloth from the front of his robe, and hung it from the tip of a bamboo pole.

What they didn't know was that in the wild mugwort at the foot of the wall, a skinny little fellow lay flat on his belly, committing those two names to memory, syllable by syllable.

By the well, the man opened his mouth and let out a long, thin sigh.

"*Aaahhh...*"

The sound drifted, faint and wavering, and circled once around the empty courtyard.

They had been working this trick for two months. In this whole stretch south of the city, not one soul dared spend a night in this house.

The new arrival today was a returned monk with a few servants. Scaring off a monk would be no trouble at all.

When the sigh was done, he cocked his head and listened.

Not a sound from inside.

He sighed again, longer, more mournful.

Still nothing.

He was about to sigh a third time when, without warning, a voice came from the main hall.

"...Zhang Xiaoyi."

The voice was low and hoarse, as if it had crawled up out of the ground, every word dragged out long.

The man by the well went rigid.

Zhang Xiaoyi was his name.

"...Zhang Xiaoyi... you've dirtied my floor..."

The voice slid left, then right. One moment it seemed to be in the main hall, the next overhead, the next right up behind his ear.

"...I've been waiting on the beam... for you lot... two months now..."

The bamboo pole dropped from Zhang Xiaoyi's hand and hit the ground with a *clack*, and the white cloth flopped over his face. He opened his mouth wide to scream, but all that squeezed out of his throat was a single *gk*.

Over at the main hall, the one who had crept inside came scrambling out on hands and knees, the crotch of his trousers soaked through, babbling. "The beam... the b-beam... there's someone..."

The one from the front courtyard came running back too. The three of them huddled together in a heap and, shaking all over, backed toward the rear wall.

They had only just reached the foot of it when—*fwip*—someone dropped into a crouch on top of the wall.

Over by the moon gate, another figure was standing.

The door of the sleeping hall opened, and two more came out.

One, two, three... ten.

Ten people, without a sound, closed in from every direction and pinned them in the corner.

In the main hall, an oil lamp slowly brightened.

Behind the lamp, Shen You sat on a folding stool, an outer robe draped over his shoulders and a bowl of hot water in his hand. He yawned.

"The three of you, this late at night," he said. "Here for the housewarming?"

***

When the three of them were marched into the main hall, their legs were still jelly.

Zhou Seven stood behind Shen You and gave them a wide grin. Out came that voice again, the one that had crawled up out of the ground. "Zhang Xiaoyi—"

Zhang Xiaoyi dropped to his knees with a thud.

It didn't take much asking. The three of them told everything.

They were layabouts who ran errands for Qian Biao. Two months ago, Qian Biao had set them to "haunting" this house at night to scare off buyers—and, while they were at it, to turn the place upside down looking for a black lacquer box, palm-sized, sealed with clay. Find it, and each of them would get ten strings.

Two months of searching, half the floor bricks pried up, and they'd found nothing. Since the house was sitting empty anyway, Qian Biao had them stash the stolen goods they had on hand here too.

"Why didn't Qian Biao just buy the house himself?" Shen You asked. "Twenty strings—a chief constable can come up with that."

"Chief Qian said buying a house means a deed and a transfer, and the county keeps a copy." Zhang Xiaoyi's face crumpled. "If anything ever went wrong with the thing, one look and it'd lead straight back to him. Better to have a ghost, so nobody'd dare buy. In the daytime he even made a point of bringing men round to circle the gate, acting too scared to go in, so folks in the ward would believe it all the more..."

"And the beam?" Shen You glanced up. "Two months of searching, and nobody thought to look on the beam?"

The color drained from Zhang Xiaoyi's face. "Th-that's the beam a man hanged himself from... Who'd dare climb up there..."

That caught Shen You off guard for a moment. Then he laughed out loud.

Two months playing ghost, and they were the ones afraid of ghosts.

"What does Qian Biao want the box for?"

"I don't know! I truly don't!" Zhang Xiaoyi kowtowed so fast his forehead hammered the floor like a pestle. "Chief Qian only said he was finding it for some important person. Which important person—the likes of us don't get to ask..."

Zhou One spread out a sheet of paper, took down what the three of them said line by line, read it back to them, and had them press their thumbprints to it.

"Master," Zhou Four asked, "what do we do with them?"

Shen You thought it over.

"Let them go."

The three men froze.

"Go back and take a message to Chief Qian for me." Shen You folded the thumbprinted confession and tucked it into the front of his robe. "The house? I'm staying put. Whatever he wants, he can come and talk it over with me himself."

***

The three of them scrambled over the wall and were gone.

Zhou One watched the top of the wall and said in a low voice, "Master, that's letting the tiger back into the mountains."

"Three cats, that's all," Shen You said. "The tiger's behind them."

He finished the bowl of hot water and got up to go to bed.

"Sleep. Tomorrow we'll be fifteen."

***

The third watch, in a small courtyard house in Xuanyang Ward.

Qian Biao sat by the lamp with his clothes thrown over his shoulders and listened to Zhang Xiaoyi stammer his way through the whole story. For a long while he said nothing.

"Ten people?"

"Ten! Off the wall, from behind the doors, out of the wing rooms—they all popped up at once..."

"A monk returned to lay life, not even a few days in Chang'an, and he buys a house, gets himself on the register, and has ten people at his back." Qian Biao's fingers tapped against the hilt of his sword, one beat at a time. "And one of them can do ghost voices."

Abruptly he stood, grabbed his *hengdao*, and pushed out the door.

The night was already very deep. He showed his waist tablet, had the Xuanyang Ward gate opened for him, and headed west, cutting through Chongyi Ward, until he stopped at the inconspicuous back door of a house. He raised his hand and knocked three times, paused, then knocked twice more.

The door opened a crack.

There was no lamp lit inside. Out of the darkness, a voice asked slowly:

"Did you find it?"

"...Someone beat us to it." Qian Biao kept his head lowered. "A monk returned to lay life. He bought the house just today. The thing's most likely in his hands."

Behind the door there was a long silence.

"That is something the Crown Prince left behind," the voice said. "It must not fall into outside hands. Still less can it be allowed to end up in Xiande Hall of the Eastern Palace."

"Understood."

"You are an official," the voice said. "And an official has an official's ways."

The door eased quietly shut.
